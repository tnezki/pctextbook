SECTION 6.A
Graph Quadratic Equations

Learning Objectives

I can graph quadratic equations by identifying the vertex, axis of symmetry, and key points on parabolas.

I can apply transformations to the parent function $y = x^2$ to sketch parabolas with vertical and horizontal shifts, reflections, and stretches.

I can use technology strategically to verify my graphs and explore the relationships between parameters in different forms of quadratic equations.

Introduction: The World of Parabolas

Quadratic equations create parabolas, one of the most important curves in mathematics and the natural world. Whether you're designing a bridge, launching a projectile, or optimizing profit, parabolas describe the situation. By learning to graph these functions, you gain a visual understanding that connects the algebraic equation to its real-world meaning.

Real-World Connection

A basketball player shoots from the free throw line. The ball follows a parabolic path, reaching its maximum height about 10 feet above the ground before falling through the net. The equation describing this path is a quadratic function. Architects use parabolic shapes in tunnels and bridges because they distribute weight efficiently. Understanding how to graph these curves helps engineers and scientists solve practical problems.

Historical Note

The ancient Greeks studied parabolas as conic sections—curves created by slicing a cone. Apollonius of Perga described these curves around 200 BCE. Centuries later, Galileo discovered that objects in projectile motion follow parabolic paths, connecting abstract mathematics to physics.

Key Vocabulary and Concepts

Parabola: A U-shaped curve created by a quadratic function. It can open upward (when $a > 0$) or downward (when $a < 0$).

Vertex: The lowest point on an upward-opening parabola or the highest point on a downward-opening parabola. This is where the parabola changes direction.

Axis of Symmetry: A vertical line that divides the parabola into two mirror-image halves. The equation of this line is $x = h$, where $h$ is the x-coordinate of the vertex.

Three Forms of Quadratic Equations

Standard Form: $y = ax^2 + bx + c$

This form makes the y-intercept easy to find: it is the value of $c$.

Vertex Form: $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$

This form shows the vertex directly: $(h, k)$ is where the parabola peaks or bottoms out.

Understanding the Parameters

  • $a$ controls the width and direction.
  • $h$ shifts the parabola left or right.
  • $k$ shifts the parabola up or down.

Examples

Example 1

Graph $y = -2x^2 + 8x - 3$ by finding the vertex and axis of symmetry.

Example 2

Graph $y = 2(x - 3)^2 - 5$ by identifying transformations from $y = x^2$.

Example 3

Rewrite $y = x^2 - 4x + 7$ in vertex form by completing the square.

Example 4

Graph $y = -\frac{1}{2}x^2 + 2x + 4$ by finding the vertex, y-intercept, and x-intercepts.

Summary of Key Concepts

  • Parabolas are created by quadratic equations.
  • The vertex is the turning point.
  • The axis of symmetry divides the graph into mirror halves.
  • Vertex form makes transformations visible.
  • Standard form reveals intercepts easily.
  • The parameter $a$ controls width and direction.

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting negative signs in $-\frac{b}{2a}$.
  • Confusing horizontal and vertical shifts.
  • Errors while completing the square.
  • Misidentifying the axis of symmetry.

Practice Problems

Easy

1. Graph $y = (x - 1)^2 + 2$. Identify the vertex and axis of symmetry.
2. For $y = -3x^2 + 6x - 1$, find the x-coordinate of the vertex.
3. Find the vertex and axis of symmetry of $y = x^2 + 4x + 1$.
4. Determine whether $y = -4(x + 2)^2 + 3$ opens upward or downward and identify its vertex.

Medium

5. Rewrite $y = 2x^2 + 12x + 5$ in vertex form.
6. The parabola $y = ax^2$ is shifted 3 units left and 4 units up. Write the equation if $a = -2$.
7. Find the vertex, axis of symmetry, and y-intercept of $y = x^2 - 6x + 8$. Then graph it.
8. Convert $y = -x^2 + 2x + 3$ to vertex form and identify the maximum value.

Hard

9. A parabola passes through $(1,5), (3,1), (5,5)$. Determine its equation in vertex form.
10. Compare $y = 2x^2$ and $y = \frac{1}{2}(x - 4)^2 - 3$. Which is narrower? How are they positioned?
11. The height of a projectile is $h(t) = -16t^2 + 64t + 32$. Find the maximum height.
12. Find the x-intercepts of $y = x^2 - 5x + 6$ and graph the parabola.

Challenge

13. Write a quadratic equation with vertex $(2, -3)$ that passes through $(0, 5)$.
14. A parabola opens downward and has x-intercepts at -2 and 6. Find its equation.
15. Without graphing technology, explain how you could determine whether a parabola has a maximum or minimum value.

Justify

16. Explain why $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$ gives the x-coordinate of the vertex.
17. Justify how completing the square reveals the vertex form.
18. Describe how changing the value of $a$ affects the shape and direction of a parabola.

Solutions

Solutions

Easy

1. The equation is already in vertex form: $y = (x - 1)^2 + 2$. Vertex: $(1, 2)$ Axis of symmetry: $x = 1$ Since $a = 1 > 0$, the parabola opens upward.

2. For $y = -3x^2 + 6x - 1$: \[ x = -\frac{b}{2a} = -\frac{6}{2(-3)} = -\frac{6}{-6} = 1 \] Substitute $x = 1$: \[ y = -3(1)^2 + 6(1) - 1 = -3 + 6 - 1 = 2 \] Vertex: $(1, 2)$

3. For $y = x^2 + 4x + 1$: \[ x = -\frac{4}{2(1)} = -2 \] \[ y = (-2)^2 + 4(-2) + 1 = 4 - 8 + 1 = -3 \] Vertex: $(-2, -3)$ Axis of symmetry: $x = -2$

4. The equation $y = -4(x + 2)^2 + 3$ is in vertex form. Vertex: $(-2, 3)$ Since $a = -4 < 0$, the parabola opens downward.

Medium

5. Rewrite $y = 2x^2 + 12x + 5$. Factor 2: \[ y = 2(x^2 + 6x) + 5 \] Complete the square: \[ (6/2)^2 = 9 \] \[ y = 2(x^2 + 6x + 9 - 9) + 5 \] \[ y = 2(x + 3)^2 - 18 + 5 \] \[ y = 2(x + 3)^2 - 13 \] Vertex: $(-3, -13)$

6. Start with $y = -2x^2$. Shift left 3 units: \[ y = -2(x + 3)^2 \] Shift up 4 units: \[ y = -2(x + 3)^2 + 4 \]

7. For $y = x^2 - 6x + 8$: \[ x = -\frac{-6}{2(1)} = 3 \] \[ y = 3^2 - 6(3) + 8 = 9 - 18 + 8 = -1 \] Vertex: $(3, -1)$ Axis of symmetry: $x = 3$ Y-intercept: $(0, 8)$

8. For $y = -x^2 + 2x + 3$: \[ x = -\frac{2}{2(-1)} = 1 \] \[ y = -(1)^2 + 2(1) + 3 = -1 + 2 + 3 = 4 \] Vertex: $(1, 4)$ Maximum value: 4

Hard

9. Points: $(1,5)$ and $(5,5)$ are symmetric, so axis of symmetry is: \[ x = \frac{1 + 5}{2} = 3 \] Vertex is $(3,1)$. Vertex form: \[ y = a(x - 3)^2 + 1 \] Use point $(1,5)$: \[ 5 = a(1 - 3)^2 + 1 \] \[ 5 = 4a + 1 \] \[ a = 1 \] Equation: \[ y = (x - 3)^2 + 1 \]

10. Both parabolas open upward since both $a$ values are positive. $y = 2x^2$ is narrower because $|2| > |\frac{1}{2}|$. The second parabola is shifted right 4 units and down 3 units.

11. For $h(t) = -16t^2 + 64t + 32$: \[ t = -\frac{64}{2(-16)} = 2 \] \[ h(2) = -16(4) + 128 + 32 = -64 + 128 + 32 = 96 \] Maximum height: 96 feet at 2 seconds.

12. Factor: \[ y = x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3) \] X-intercepts: 2 and 3. Vertex: \[ x = -\frac{-5}{2(1)} = 2.5 \] \[ y = (2.5)^2 - 5(2.5) + 6 = -0.25 \] Vertex: $(2.5, -0.25)$

Challenge

13. Vertex form: \[ y = a(x - 2)^2 - 3 \] Use point $(0,5)$: \[ 5 = a(4) - 3 \] \[ a = 2 \] Equation: \[ y = 2(x - 2)^2 - 3 \]

14. Factored form: \[ y = a(x + 2)(x - 6) \] Since it opens downward, choose $a = -1$: \[ y = -(x + 2)(x - 6) \]

15. If $a > 0$, the parabola has a minimum. If $a < 0$, the parabola has a maximum.

Justify

16. The formula $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$ comes from completing the square on the standard form $ax^2 + bx + c$.

17. Completing the square rewrites a quadratic into vertex form $a(x - h)^2 + k$, which directly reveals the vertex.

18. The value of $a$ controls direction and width. If $a > 0$, the parabola opens upward. If $a < 0$, it opens downward. Larger $|a|$ makes the parabola narrower.
