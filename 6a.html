SECTION 6.A

Graph Quadratic Equations

Parabolas are everywhere—from the arc of a basketball to the path of a satellite. In this section, you'll learn how to graph quadratic equations by identifying their key features: the vertex, axis of symmetry, and important points. You'll discover how changing the equation transforms the parabola and why the parameters $a$, $h$, and $k$ tell a story about the graph.

Learning Objectives

  • I can graph quadratic equations by identifying the vertex, axis of symmetry, and key points on parabolas.
  • I can apply transformations to the parent function $y = x^2$ to sketch parabolas with vertical and horizontal shifts, reflections, and stretches.
  • I can use technology strategically to verify my graphs and explore the relationships between parameters in different forms of quadratic equations.

Introduction: The World of Parabolas

Quadratic equations create parabolas, one of the most important curves in mathematics and the natural world. Whether you're designing a bridge, launching a projectile, or optimizing profit, parabolas describe the situation. By learning to graph these functions, you gain a visual understanding that connects the algebraic equation to its real-world meaning.

Real-World Connection

A basketball player shoots from the free throw line. The ball follows a parabolic path, reaching its maximum height about 10 feet above the ground before falling through the net. The equation describing this path is a quadratic function. Architects use parabolic shapes in tunnels and bridges because they distribute weight efficiently. Understanding how to graph these curves helps engineers and scientists solve practical problems.

Historical Note: The ancient Greeks studied parabolas as conic sections—curves created by slicing a cone. Apollonius of Perga described these curves around 200 BCE. Centuries later, Galileo discovered that objects in projectile motion follow parabolic paths, connecting abstract mathematics to physics.

Key Vocabulary and Concepts

Parabola: A U-shaped curve created by a quadratic function. It can open upward (when $a > 0$) or downward (when $a < 0$).
Vertex: The lowest point on an upward-opening parabola or the highest point on a downward-opening parabola. This is where the parabola changes direction.
Axis of Symmetry: A vertical line that divides the parabola into two mirror-image halves. The equation of this line is $x = h$, where $h$ is the x-coordinate of the vertex.

Three Forms of Quadratic Equations

Quadratic equations can be written in different forms, each revealing different information about the parabola:

Standard Form: $$y = ax^2 + bx + c$$

This form makes the y-intercept easy to find: it's the value of $c$.

Vertex Form: $$y = a(x - h)^2 + k$$

This form shows the vertex directly: $(h, k)$ is where the parabola peaks or bottoms out.

Understanding the Parameters

In vertex form $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$, each parameter tells you something specific:

  • $a$: Controls the "width" and direction. If $a > 0$, the parabola opens up. If $a < 0$, it opens down. Larger $|a|$ makes it narrower.
  • $h$: Shifts the parabola left or right. The vertex moves to $(h, k)$.
  • $k$: Shifts the parabola up or down. The vertex moves to $(h, k)$.

Example Problems

Example 1: Finding the Vertex and Axis of Symmetry from Standard Form

Problem: Graph $y = -2x^2 + 8x - 3$ by finding the vertex and axis of symmetry.

Solution: First, identify $a = -2$, $b = 8$, and $c = -3$.

Find the x-coordinate of the vertex using the formula $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$:

$$x = -\frac{8}{2(-2)} = -\frac{8}{-4} = 2$$

The axis of symmetry is $x = 2$.

Find the y-coordinate by substituting $x = 2$ into the equation:

$$y = -2(2)^2 + 8(2) - 3 = -2(4) + 16 - 3 = -8 + 16 - 3 = 5$$

Vertex: $(2, 5)$ and Axis of Symmetry: $x = 2$

Since $a = -2 < 0$, the parabola opens downward with its maximum point at $(2, 5)$.

Example 2: Graphing Using Vertex Form

Problem: Graph $y = 2(x - 3)^2 - 5$ by identifying transformations of $y = x^2$.

Solution: The equation is already in vertex form $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$ where $a = 2$, $h = 3$, and $k = -5$.

This tells us that $y = x^2$ has been transformed by:

  • Shifted right 3 units (because of $x - 3$)
  • Shifted down 5 units (because of $-5$)
  • Stretched vertically by a factor of 2 (because $a = 2$)

Vertex: $(3, -5)$

Axis of Symmetry: $x = 3$

Since $a = 2 > 0$, the parabola opens upward. To graph, plot the vertex and a few more points symmetric about $x = 3$:

  • When $x = 2$: $y = 2(2 - 3)^2 - 5 = 2(1) - 5 = -3$, giving point $(2, -3)$
  • When $x = 4$: $y = 2(4 - 3)^2 - 5 = 2(1) - 5 = -3$, giving point $(4, -3)$

Example 3: Converting from Standard to Vertex Form

Problem: Rewrite $y = x^2 - 4x + 7$ in vertex form by completing the square.

Solution: To complete the square, group the $x$ terms and find the constant to add/subtract.

Take half of the coefficient of $x$ and square it: $\left(\frac{-4}{2}\right)^2 = (-2)^2 = 4$

$$y = x^2 - 4x + 7$$ $$y = (x^2 - 4x + 4) - 4 + 7$$ $$y = (x - 2)^2 + 3$$

Vertex form: $y = (x - 2)^2 + 3$

Vertex: $(2, 3)$ and Axis of Symmetry: $x = 2$

The parabola opens upward and has been shifted right 2 units and up 3 units from the parent function $y = x^2$.

Example 4: Finding Key Points on a Parabola

Problem: Graph $y = -\frac{1}{2}x^2 + 2x + 4$ by finding the vertex, y-intercept, and x-intercepts.

Solution: First, find the vertex using $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$ where $a = -\frac{1}{2}$, $b = 2$, and $c = 4$.

$$x = -\frac{2}{2(-\frac{1}{2})} = -\frac{2}{-1} = 2$$

Substitute $x = 2$:

$$y = -\frac{1}{2}(2)^2 + 2(2) + 4 = -\frac{1}{2}(4) + 4 + 4 = -2 + 8 = 6$$

Vertex: $(2, 6)$

Y-intercept: Set $x = 0$: $y = 4$, giving point $(0, 4)$

X-intercepts: Set $y = 0$ and solve: $0 = -\frac{1}{2}x^2 + 2x + 4$

Multiply by $-2$: $0 = x^2 - 4x - 8$

Using the quadratic formula: $x = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 + 32}}{2} = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{48}}{2} = \frac{4 \pm 4\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2 \pm 2\sqrt{3}$

X-intercepts: $(2 - 2\sqrt{3}, 0)$ and $(2 + 2\sqrt{3}, 0)$, or approximately $(-1.46, 0)$ and $(5.46, 0)$

Since $a = -\frac{1}{2} < 0$, the parabola opens downward with its maximum at $(2, 6)$.

Summary of Key Concepts

  • Parabolas are U-shaped curves created by quadratic functions. They open upward when $a > 0$ and downward when $a < 0$.
  • The vertex is the turning point of the parabola, found at $(h, k)$ in vertex form or using $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$ in standard form.
  • The axis of symmetry is the vertical line $x = h$ (or $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$) that divides the parabola into two symmetric halves.
  • Vertex form $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$ directly shows the vertex and makes transformations clear.
  • Standard form $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ makes the y-intercept obvious and is useful for finding intercepts.
  • The parameter $a$ controls the width and direction: larger $|a|$ makes the parabola narrower, and the sign determines whether it opens up or down.
  • Transformations of $y = x^2$ include horizontal shifts ($h$), vertical shifts ($k$), reflections (negative $a$), and stretches/compressions ($|a|$).
  • Technology like graphing calculators and software can verify your hand-drawn graphs and help you explore how changing parameters affects the parabola.

Key Equations and Forms

Practice Problems

Problem 1 (Easy): Graph $y = (x - 1)^2 + 2$. Identify the vertex and axis of symmetry.
Problem 2 (Easy): For the parabola $y = -3x^2 + 6x - 1$, find the x-coordinate of the vertex using $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$.
Problem 3 (Medium): Rewrite $y = 2x^2 + 12x + 5$ in vertex form by completing the square.
Problem 4 (Medium): The parabola $y = ax^2$ is shifted 3 units left and 4 units up. Write the equation in vertex form if $a = -2$.
Problem 5 (Medium): Find the vertex, axis of symmetry, and y-intercept of $y = x^2 - 6x + 8$.
Problem 6 (Harder): A parabola passes through the points $(1, 5)$, $(3, 1)$, and $(5, 5)$. Determine the equation in vertex form, identifying the vertex first.
Problem 7 (Harder): Compare the parabolas $y = 2x^2$ and $y = \frac{1}{2}(x - 4)^2 - 3$. Which opens upward? Which is narrower? How are they positioned relative to each other?
Problem 8 (Harder): The height of a projectile is given by $h(t) = -16t^2 + 64t + 32$, where $t$ is time in seconds and $h$ is height in feet. Find the maximum height and the time when it occurs.

Answer Key

Answer 1: Vertex: $(1, 2)$; Axis of Symmetry: $x = 1$. The parabola opens upward and has been shifted right 1 unit and up 2 units from $y = x^2$.
Answer 2: $x = -\frac{6}{2(-3)} = -\frac{6}{-6} = 1$. The x-coordinate of the vertex is 1.
Answer 3: Start with $y = 2x^2 + 12x + 5$. Factor out 2 from the $x$ terms: $y = 2(x^2 + 6x) + 5$. Complete the square inside the parentheses: $(6/2)^2 = 9$. So $y = 2(x^2 + 6x + 9 - 9) + 5 = 2((x + 3)^2 - 9) + 5 = 2(x + 3)^2 - 18 + 5 = 2(x + 3)^2 - 13$. The vertex form is $y = 2(x + 3)^2 - 13$.
Answer 4: Start with $y = -2x^2$. Shift left 3 units: $y = -2(x + 3)^2$. Shift up 4 units: $y = -2(x + 3)^2 + 4$. This is the equation in vertex form.
Answer 5: For $y = x^2 - 6x + 8$, use $x = -\frac{b}{2a} = -\frac{-6}{2(1)} = 3$. When $x = 3$: $y = (3)^2 - 6(3) + 8 = 9 - 18 + 8 = -1$. Vertex: $(3, -1)$; Axis of Symmetry: $x = 3$; Y-intercept: $(0, 8)$ (set $x = 0$: $y = 8$).
Answer 6: The parabola is symmetric about its vertex. Notice that $(1, 5)$ and $(5, 5)$ have the same y-value, so the axis of symmetry is at $x = \frac{1 + 5}{2} = 3$. The vertex x-coordinate is 3. At $x = 3$, from the point $(3, 1)$, we have $k = 1$, so the vertex is $(3, 1)$. Using the vertex form $y = a(x - 3)^2 + 1$ and the point $(1, 5)$: $5 = a(1 - 3)^2 + 1 = 4a + 1$, so $4a = 4$ and $a = 1$. The equation is $y = (x - 3)^2 + 1$.
Answer 7: Both open upward (both have positive $a$). The parabola $y = 2x^2$ is narrower because $|2| > |1/2|$. The parabola $y = 2x^2$ has its vertex at the origin $(0, 0)$, while $y = \frac{1}{2}(x - 4)^2 - 3$ has its vertex at $(4, -3)$. The second parabola is shifted right 4 units and down 3 units from the first.
Answer 8: For $h(t) = -16t^2 + 64t + 32$, find the vertex using $t = -\frac{b}{2a} = -\frac{64}{2(-16)} = -\frac{64}{-32} = 2$ seconds. At $t = 2$: $h(2) = -16(2)^2 + 64(2) + 32 = -16(4) + 128 + 32 = -64 + 128 + 32 = 96$ feet. The maximum height is 96 feet, occurring at $t = 2$ seconds.
