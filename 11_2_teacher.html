Intro

Directions

Read the introduction aloud with a partner or in a small group. As you read, annotate the text by marking important ideas, circling key vocabulary, and writing short notes about connections, questions, or predictions in the margins.

Up to this point, derivatives of sums have been manageable because each term could be differentiated separately. This section begins with a different kind of structure: multiplication. When two functions are multiplied together, the derivative cannot be found by differentiating each factor independently and then multiplying those results. The product rule is the shortcut that handles that situation and shows how the rate of change of a product depends on both factors.

This kind of reasoning connects naturally to science and engineering, where changing quantities are often linked together rather than added separately. In physics, formulas for motion, force, and energy often involve products of variables. In computer science and data analysis, combined quantities frequently depend on more than one changing component at a time. The product rule reflects that same idea mathematically: when parts interact through multiplication, their rates of change interact too.

Product relationships also appear in real-world models. The area of a rectangle depends on the product of length and width. Quantities in periodic motion can combine algebraic growth with trigonometric behavior. A function like $x^2\sin x$ combines a steadily changing polynomial factor with a repeating trigonometric factor, creating a model whose rate of change depends on both parts. The product rule gives a way to describe that combined change.

Historically, the product rule emerged as calculus developed into a system for working with increasingly complicated functions. Once mathematicians understood basic derivatives of powers and trigonometric functions, they needed rules that explained how derivatives behave when functions are combined. The product rule became one of the foundational derivative rules because it answered that question for multiplication and opened the door to more realistic models and more complex expressions.

These symbols all represent derivative notation. In this section, you will also see product notation such as $(u)(v)$ and factors written side by side.

When the Product Rule Is Needed

The product rule is used when a function is formed by multiplying two functions together. If the parts are being added or subtracted, the product rule is not needed. The first step is always to identify the structure correctly.

Look for Two Factors

  • $x^2+\sin x$ is a sum.
  • $x^2\sin x$ is a product.
  • $(x^3)(\cos x)$ is a product.

Example 1

State whether the product rule is needed for each function.

  1. $x^3+\cos x$
  2. $x^3\cos x$
  3. $(x^2)(\sin x)$

Answer: The product rule is not needed for $x^3+\cos x$ because it is a sum. The product rule is needed for $x^3\cos x$ and $(x^2)(\sin x)$ because each is a product of two functions.

You Try It 1

State whether the product rule is needed for each function.

  1. $x^4-\sin x$
  2. $(x^4+1)\sin x$
  3. $\cos x(x+3)$

Answer: The product rule is not needed for $x^4-\sin x$. The product rule is needed for $(x^4+1)\sin x$ and $\cos x(x+3)$.

Stop and Discuss

How can you tell whether a function should be handled with the product rule or by differentiating term by term?

Answer: The product rule is used when two functions are multiplied together. If the structure is a sum or difference, the terms can be differentiated separately.

The Product Rule

Product Rule

\[ \frac{d}{dx}[u(x)v(x)] = u'(x)v(x)+u(x)v'(x) \]

Differentiate the first factor and leave the second alone, then add the first factor times the derivative of the second.

The product rule shows that the derivative of a product comes from two contributions: one from the first factor changing and one from the second factor changing. Both factors matter, so the derivative must contain two terms.

Example 2

Find the derivative of $f(x)=x^2\sin x$.

Answer: Let $u=x^2$ and $v=\sin x$.

\[ u'=2x \quad \text{and} \quad v'=\cos x \] \[ f'(x)=u'v+uv'=(2x)(\sin x)+(x^2)(\cos x) \] \[ f'(x)=2x\sin x+x^2\cos x \]

You Try It 2

Find the derivative of $g(x)=x^3\cos x$.

Answer: Let $u=x^3$ and $v=\cos x$.

\[ u'=3x^2 \quad \text{and} \quad v'=-\sin x \] \[ g'(x)=u'v+uv'=(3x^2)(\cos x)+(x^3)(-\sin x) \] \[ g'(x)=3x^2\cos x-x^3\sin x \]

Stop and Discuss

Why does the product rule have two terms instead of one?

Answer: The rule has two terms because both factors are changing and each factor contributes to the overall rate of change of the product.

Combining the Product Rule with Other Derivative Rules

The product rule does not replace the power rule or the trig derivative facts. Instead, it works together with them. Each factor is differentiated using the rule that matches its structure.

Strategy

  1. Identify the two factors.
  2. Differentiate each factor using the correct rule.
  3. Substitute into the product rule.
  4. Simplify the result.

Example 3

Find the derivative of $h(x)=(2x^3-x+4)\sin x$.

Answer: Let $u=2x^3-x+4$ and $v=\sin x$.

\[ u'=6x^2-1 \quad \text{and} \quad v'=\cos x \] \[ h'(x)=u'v+uv'=(6x^2-1)\sin x+(2x^3-x+4)\cos x \]

You Try It 3

Find the derivative of $p(x)=\cos x(x^2-3x+2)$.

Answer:

\[ p'(x)=-\sin x(x^2-3x+2)+\cos x(2x-3) \]

Stop and Discuss

How do the power rule and the basic trigonometric derivatives show up inside a product-rule problem?

Answer: Each factor is differentiated with the rule that fits it. Polynomial factors use the power rule, trig factors use their basic derivative facts, and those results are then combined with the product rule.

Evaluating a Product-Rule Derivative at a Given Value

Sometimes the derivative expression is only the first step. After finding the derivative, the next task may be to evaluate it at a specific input value.

In product-rule problems, that means the structure still matters first. It is usually better to find the full derivative expression, simplify it if needed, and only then substitute the given value.

Example 4

For $q(x)=(x^2+1)\sin x$, find $q'(x)$ and then evaluate $q'(0)$.

Answer:

\[ q'(x)=2x\sin x+(x^2+1)\cos x \] \[ q'(0)=2(0)\sin 0+((0)^2+1)\cos 0=1 \]

You Try It 4

For $r(x)=\cos x(x^2-1)$, find $r'(x)$ and then evaluate $r'(0)$.

Answer:

\[ r'(x)=-\sin x(x^2-1)+2x\cos x \] \[ r'(0)=0 \]

Stop and Discuss

Why should the derivative expression be found before substituting a specific value?

Answer: The derivative must describe how the full product changes. Finding that expression first preserves the structure of the rule and makes substitution more reliable.

Summary Concepts

  • The product rule is needed when two functions are multiplied together.
  • The product rule gives a way to differentiate products by accounting for how both factors change.
  • Each factor is differentiated using the rule that fits its structure.
  • After finding the derivative, a given $x$ value can be substituted into the derivative expression.
  • The product rule works together with the power rule and the basic trigonometric derivatives.

Common Mistakes

  • Treating a product like a sum and differentiating only one factor.
  • Forgetting the second term in $u'v+uv'$.
  • Forgetting that $\dfrac{d}{dx}(\cos x)=-\sin x$.
  • Substituting a value before finding the derivative expression.

Detailed Example Solutions

Example 1 Solution

State whether the product rule is needed for each function.

  1. $x^3+\cos x$
  2. $x^3\cos x$
  3. $(x^2)(\sin x)$

$x^3+\cos x$ is a sum, so the product rule is not needed.

$x^3\cos x$ and $(x^2)(\sin x)$ are products, so the product rule is needed for both.

Example 2 Solution

Find the derivative of $f(x)=x^2\sin x$.

Let $u=x^2$ and $v=\sin x$.

\[ u'=2x \quad \text{and} \quad v'=\cos x \]

Apply the product rule.

\[ f'(x)=u'v+uv'=(2x)(\sin x)+(x^2)(\cos x) \] \[ f'(x)=2x\sin x+x^2\cos x \]

Example 3 Solution

Find the derivative of $h(x)=(2x^3-x+4)\sin x$.

Differentiate the polynomial factor with the power rule and the trig factor with the basic trig derivative.

\[ h'(x)=(6x^2-1)\sin x+(2x^3-x+4)\cos x \]

Example 4 Solution

For $q(x)=(x^2+1)\sin x$, find $q'(x)$ and evaluate $q'(0)$.

\[ q'(x)=2x\sin x+(x^2+1)\cos x \] \[ q'(0)=2(0)\sin 0+((0)^2+1)\cos 0=1 \]
