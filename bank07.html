Section 7.B — Double-Angle & Pythagorean Identities

Objective B: I can apply double-angle and Pythagorean identities to simplify and rewrite trigonometric expressions while justifying each algebraic step.

1. Simplify completely: $\frac{1 - \cos(2\theta)}{\sin^2\theta}$

2. Rewrite $\cos(2\theta)$ in terms of sine only.

3. Rewrite $\cos(2\theta)$ in terms of cosine only.

4. Simplify $\frac{\sin^2\theta}{1 - \cos^2\theta}$.

5. Simplify $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta$.

6. If $\sin\theta = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\theta$ is in Quadrant I, find $\sin(2\theta)$.

7. If $\cos\theta = \frac{4}{5}$ and $\theta$ is in Quadrant I, find $\cos(2\theta)$.

8. Simplify $\frac{1 - \cos(2\theta)}{2}$.

9. Simplify $\frac{1 + \cos(2\theta)}{2}$.

10. Rewrite $\sin(2\theta)$ in terms of sine and cosine.

11. Simplify $\frac{\tan^2\theta + 1}{\sec^2\theta}$.

Section 7.C — Half-Angle, Sum, and Difference Identities

Objective C: I can apply half-angle formulas and sum/difference identities to rewrite and evaluate trigonometric expressions with correct sign determination and algebraic precision.

1. Evaluate $\cos\left(\frac{3\pi}{8}\right)$ exactly.

2. Evaluate $\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{12}\right)$ exactly.

3. Evaluate $\cos(105^\circ)$ exactly.

4. Rewrite $\sin(60^\circ - 45^\circ)$.

5. Rewrite $\cos(45^\circ + 30^\circ)$.

6. Simplify $\cos(A - B) + \cos(A + B)$.

7. Simplify $\sin(A + B) - \sin(A - B)$.

8. If $\cos\theta = \frac{1}{3}$ and $\theta$ is in Quadrant II, evaluate $\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)$.

9. If $\sin\theta = -\frac{4}{5}$ and $\theta$ is in Quadrant III, evaluate $\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)$.

10. Rewrite $\sin(75^\circ)$ using angle sum identities.

11. Evaluate $\tan\left(\frac{\pi}{8}\right)$ exactly.

Objective D: I can solve advanced trigonometric equations in radians by isolating trig functions, identifying reference angles, determining all solutions in an interval, and writing general solutions when required.

2. Solve $\cos\theta = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

6. Solve $\sec\theta = 2$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

7. Solve $\csc\theta = -\sqrt{2}$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

10. Solve $\cos(2\theta) = 0$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

Section 7.E — Riemann and Trapezoidal Approximations

Objective E: I can approximate the area under a curve using left and right Riemann sums and trapezoidal sums from functions and intervals, and I can justify which method provides a better estimate.

1. For $f(x) = x^2 + 2$ on $[1,5]$, compute:
(a) a left Riemann sum with 4 subintervals,
(b) a trapezoidal approximation with 4 subintervals.
Which method appears more accurate? Justify.

2. Approximate the area under $f(x) = \sqrt{x+1}$ on $[2,6]$ using a left Riemann sum with 4 subintervals.

3. Approximate the area under $f(x) = x^3 - 1$ on $[0,4]$ using a right Riemann sum with 4 subintervals.

4. Approximate the area under $f(x) = \sin x$ on $\left[\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{5\pi}{4}\right]$ using a trapezoidal sum with 4 subintervals.

5. Approximate the area under $f(x) = x^2 - 3x$ on $[1,5]$ using a left Riemann sum with 4 subintervals.

6. Approximate the area under $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$ on $[3,7]$ using a right Riemann sum with 4 subintervals.

7. Approximate the area under $f(x) = x^3 - 2x$ on $[-1,3]$ using a trapezoidal sum with 4 subintervals.

8. For $f(x) = \cos x$ on $\left[0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right]$, compute a left Riemann sum with 4 subintervals.

9. For $f(x) = \sqrt{x+4}$ on $[0,4]$, compute a trapezoidal approximation with 4 subintervals.

10. Approximate the area under $f(x) = x^2 + x$ on $[2,6]$ using a right Riemann sum with 4 subintervals.

11. Compare left and right Riemann sums for $f(x) = x^2$ on $[1,5]$. Which provides the larger estimate? Explain without fully computing both.
