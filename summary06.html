MODULE 6 SUMMARY

Polynomial Functions: Structure, Behavior, and Modeling

Big Picture

Polynomial functions are structured, predictable systems. By analyzing degree, leading coefficient, roots, multiplicity, symmetry, and long-term behavior, we can sketch graphs, write equations from structure, and model real-world situations. Understanding structure leads to mathematical control.

Essential Standard

Students will analyze, graph, and construct polynomial functions by identifying key features including degree, leading coefficient, roots, multiplicity, end behavior, turning points, symmetry, and domain/range, and will apply structured reasoning to model and interpret real-world situations.

Top 10 Core Concepts & Vocabulary

Concept What It Means Why It Matters
Degree Highest power of $x$ Determines overall shape and maximum turning points
Leading Coefficient Coefficient of highest power term Determines direction of end behavior
End Behavior Graph direction as $x \to \pm\infty$ Predicts long-term behavior
Root (Zero) $x$ such that $f(x)=0$ Where graph crosses or touches x-axis
Multiplicity Number of times a root repeats Determines crossing vs bouncing behavior
Factored Form $a(x - r_1)(x - r_2)\cdots$ Reveals roots directly
Turning Points Local maximum or minimum At most $n - 1$ for degree $n$
Even Function $f(-x)=f(x)$ Symmetric about the y-axis
Odd Function $f(-x)=-f(x)$ Symmetric about the origin
Sigma Notation $\sum_{i=a}^{b} f(i)$ Represents structured accumulation

Objective A — Graph Quadratics & Vertex Structure

I can graph quadratic equations by identifying the vertex, axis of symmetry, intercepts, and direction of opening.

Key Vocabulary

  • Quadratic function
  • Vertex
  • Axis of symmetry
  • Standard form
  • Vertex form
  • Maximum / Minimum

Key Formulas

Standard form:

$y = ax^2 + bx + c$

Vertex formula:

$x = -\frac{b}{2a}$

Vertex form:

$y = a(x - h)^2 + k$

Highlights

  • If $a > 0$, the parabola opens upward.
  • If $a < 0$, the parabola opens downward.
  • The vertex is the maximum or minimum point.
  • The axis of symmetry is $x = h$ in vertex form.

Sample Questions

1. Find the vertex of $f(x) = -2x^2 + 8x - 6$.

2. Convert $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5$ to vertex form.

3. Determine whether $f(x) = 3(x - 2)^2 + 1$ has a maximum or minimum.

4. Identify the axis of symmetry of $f(x) = 2x^2 - 4x + 1$.

Objective B — Factored Form & Multiplicity

I can analyze and sketch polynomial functions written in factored form by identifying roots, multiplicity, and end behavior.

Key Vocabulary

  • Factored form
  • Root (zero)
  • Multiplicity
  • End behavior
  • Leading coefficient

Key Structure

If roots are $r_1, r_2, \dots, r_n$, then:

$f(x) = a(x - r_1)(x - r_2)\cdots(x - r_n)$

Multiplicity Rules

  • Odd multiplicity → graph crosses the x-axis.
  • Even multiplicity → graph touches and bounces.
  • Total multiplicities = degree of the polynomial.

End Behavior Reminder

  • Even degree + positive leading coefficient → both ends up.
  • Even degree + negative leading coefficient → both ends down.
  • Odd degree + positive leading coefficient → down-left, up-right.
  • Odd degree + negative leading coefficient → up-left, down-right.

Sample Questions

1. Describe the behavior of $f(x) = (x - 1)^2(x + 3)$ at each root.

2. Determine the degree of $f(x) = -2(x - 4)^3(x + 1)$.

3. Sketch the end behavior of $f(x) = -x^4(x - 2)$.

4. Identify multiplicity and crossing behavior of $f(x) = (x + 2)^4$.

Objective C — Writing Equations from Structure

I can construct polynomial equations from given intercepts, multiplicities, and additional information such as a known point.

Key Vocabulary

  • Factored form
  • Leading coefficient ($a$)
  • X-intercept
  • Y-intercept
  • Multiplicity
  • Sign behavior

Core Structure

If roots are $r_1, r_2, \dots, r_n$, then:

$f(x) = a(x - r_1)(x - r_2)\cdots(x - r_n)$

Use multiplicity when roots repeat.

Solving for the Leading Coefficient

Use a known point (often the y-intercept) to solve for $a$.

Substitute the point into the factored form and solve algebraically.

Highlights

  • Always include $a$ until you solve for it.
  • Use multiplicity correctly when the graph bounces.
  • Check that total multiplicities match the degree.
  • Substitute carefully when solving for $a$.

Sample Questions

1. A polynomial has roots at $x = -1$ and $x = 4$. Write its factored form with unknown $a$.

2. A graph crosses at $x = -3$ and touches at $x = 2$. Write a possible equation.

3. A quadratic has x-intercepts at 1 and 5 and passes through $(0, -10)$. Find the equation.

4. A polynomial has roots at $x = -2$ (multiplicity 2) and $x = 3$. What is the minimum possible degree?

Objective D — Complete Polynomial Analysis

I can analyze a polynomial function completely by identifying all key features and interpreting its behavior.

Key Vocabulary

  • Degree
  • Leading coefficient
  • End behavior
  • Roots
  • Multiplicity
  • Turning points
  • Domain
  • Range
  • Symmetry

Analysis Checklist

  • Identify the degree.
  • Identify the leading coefficient.
  • Determine end behavior.
  • Find and factor roots.
  • Identify multiplicity at each root.
  • Determine maximum turning points ($n - 1$).
  • Identify symmetry using $f(-x)$.
  • State the domain and range.

Key Reminders

  • Odd degree → ends go in opposite directions.
  • Even degree → ends go in the same direction.
  • Domain of all polynomials is all real numbers.
  • Odd-degree polynomials have range of all real numbers.
  • Even-degree polynomials may have restricted range.
  • Symmetry depends on structure, not just degree.

Sample Questions

1. Analyze $f(x) = 2x^4 - 8x^2$ completely.

2. A polynomial has degree 5 and negative leading coefficient. Describe its end behavior.

3. A graph crosses at $x = -2$ and $x = 1$ and bounces at $x = 3$. What is the minimum possible degree?

4. Determine whether $f(x) = x^4 - 3x^2$ is even, odd, or neither.

Objective E — Above & Beyond: Structured Accumulation

I can evaluate and interpret sigma notation as a representation of structured accumulation.

Key Vocabulary

  • Sigma notation ($\Sigma$)
  • Index
  • Lower bound
  • Upper bound
  • Accumulation

Core Structure

$\sum_{i=a}^{b} f(i)$

This means:

Add $f(i)$ as $i$ increases from $a$ to $b$.

Summation Properties

  • $\sum (f(i) + g(i)) = \sum f(i) + \sum g(i)$
  • $\sum c\,f(i) = c \sum f(i)$
  • The bounds determine how many terms are included.

Highlights

  • Sigma notation compresses repeated addition.
  • It represents structured accumulation over an interval.
  • Break complex expressions into separate sums before simplifying.
  • This notation prepares us to represent accumulated quantities later in the course.

Sample Questions

1. Evaluate $\sum_{i=1}^{5} i^2$.

2. Write $3 + 6 + 9 + 12 + 15$ using sigma notation.

3. Let $f(x) = x^2 - 3x$. Evaluate $\sum_{i=0}^{5} f(2i)$.

4. Explain what the bounds in $\sum_{i=2}^{6} f(i)$ tell you about the total being calculated.
