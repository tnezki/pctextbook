Section 8.2 — Product Rule

Round Table

Problem 1

Decide whether the product rule is needed for each expression: \(x^2+\sin x\) and \((x^2+1)\sin x\).

Solution 1

Look at the structure of each expression.

\(x^2+\sin x\) is a sum, so the product rule is not needed.

\((x^2+1)\sin x\) is a product of two functions, so the product rule is needed.

Problem 2

Find the derivative of \(f(x)=x^2\sin x\).

Solution 2

Let \(u=x^2\) and \(v=\sin x\).

\[ u'=2x \quad \text{and} \quad v'=\cos x \]

Apply the product rule \(u'v+uv'\).

\[ f'(x)=(2x)(\sin x)+(x^2)(\cos x) \] \[ f'(x)=2x\sin x+x^2\cos x \]
Problem 3

Find the derivative of \(g(x)=\cos x(x^2-3x+2)\).

Solution 3

Let \(u=\cos x\) and \(v=x^2-3x+2\).

\[ u'=-\sin x \quad \text{and} \quad v'=2x-3 \]

Use the product rule.

\[ g'(x)=(-\sin x)(x^2-3x+2)+(\cos x)(2x-3) \] \[ g'(x)=-\sin x(x^2-3x+2)+\cos x(2x-3) \]
Problem 4

If \(q(x)=(x^2+1)\sin x\), find \(q'(x)\) and then evaluate \(q'(0)\).

Solution 4

Differentiate first.

\[ q'(x)=2x\sin x+(x^2+1)\cos x \]

Now substitute \(x=0\).

\[ q'(0)=2(0)\sin 0+((0)^2+1)\cos 0=1 \]

So \(q'(0)=1\).

Problem 5

Find the derivative of \(h(x)=(3x^2-x+1)\sin x\).

Solution 5

Differentiate the polynomial factor and the trig factor separately.

\[ \frac{d}{dx}(3x^2-x+1)=6x-1 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{d}{dx}(\sin x)=\cos x \]

Apply the product rule.

\[ h'(x)=(6x-1)\sin x+(3x^2-x+1)\cos x \]
Problem 6

A student says, \"The derivative of \(x^3\cos x\) is \(3x^2(-\sin x)\).\" Correct the mistake.

Solution 6

The student multiplied the derivatives of the two factors instead of using the product rule.

For \(x^3\cos x\), we need

\[ \frac{d}{dx}(x^3\cos x)=(3x^2)(\cos x)+(x^3)(-\sin x) \] \[ =3x^2\cos x-x^3\sin x \]

The missing idea is that the derivative of a product has two terms.

Problem 7

Find the derivative of \(m(x)=x\cos x\).

Solution 7

Let \(u=x\) and \(v=\cos x\).

\[ u'=1 \quad \text{and} \quad v'=-\sin x \] \[ m'(x)=u'v+uv'=(1)(\cos x)+x(-\sin x) \] \[ m'(x)=\cos x-x\sin x \]
Problem 8

If \(p(x)=x^3\cos x\), find \(p'(x)\) and then evaluate \(p'(0)\).

Solution 8

Differentiate with the product rule.

\[ p'(x)=3x^2\cos x-x^3\sin x \]

Now evaluate at \(x=0\).

\[ p'(0)=3(0)^2\cos 0-(0)^3\sin 0=0 \]

So the derivative value is \(0\).

Problem 9

Find the derivative of \(r(x)=x^{1/2}\sin x\).

Solution 9

Let \(u=x^{1/2}\) and \(v=\sin x\).

\[ u'=\frac12x^{-1/2} \quad \text{and} \quad v'=\cos x \]

Apply the product rule.

\[ r'(x)=\left(\frac12x^{-1/2}\right)\sin x+x^{1/2}\cos x \]
Problem 10

Find the derivative of \(s(x)=(2x^3-x+4)\cos x\).

Solution 10

Let \(u=2x^3-x+4\) and \(v=\cos x\).

\[ u'=6x^2-1 \quad \text{and} \quad v'=-\sin x \] \[ s'(x)=(6x^2-1)\cos x+(2x^3-x+4)(-\sin x) \] \[ s'(x)=(6x^2-1)\cos x-(2x^3-x+4)\sin x \]
Problem 11

Compare the derivatives of \(a(x)=x^2+\sin x\) and \(b(x)=x^2\sin x\).

Solution 11

For the sum, differentiate term by term.

\[ a'(x)=2x+\cos x \]

For the product, use the product rule.

\[ b'(x)=2x\sin x+x^2\cos x \]

The two expressions have different derivatives because one is a sum and the other is a product.

Problem 12

Find the derivative of \(t(x)=\cos x(x^2-1)\), then evaluate \(t'(0)\).

Solution 12
\[ t'(x)=-\sin x(x^2-1)+\cos x(2x) \]

Evaluate at \(x=0\).

\[ t'(0)=-\sin 0((0)^2-1)+\cos 0(2\cdot 0)=0 \]

So \(t'(0)=0\).

Problem 13

A position function is \(s(t)=t^2\sin t\). Find the velocity function \(v(t)=s'(t)\), then find \(v(0)\).

Solution 13

Velocity is the derivative of position.

\[ v(t)=s'(t)=2t\sin t+t^2\cos t \]

Now evaluate at \(t=0\).

\[ v(0)=2(0)\sin 0+(0)^2\cos 0=0 \]

The velocity at \(t=0\) is \(0\).

Problem 14

Find the derivative of \(u(x)=(x^{-1}+x^2)\cos x\). Leave your answer with exponents.

Solution 14

Differentiate the first factor term by term.

\[ \frac{d}{dx}(x^{-1}+x^2)=-x^{-2}+2x \]

Now use the product rule.

\[ u'(x)=(-x^{-2}+2x)\cos x+(x^{-1}+x^2)(-\sin x) \] \[ u'(x)=(-x^{-2}+2x)\cos x-(x^{-1}+x^2)\sin x \]
Problem 15

A student says, \"To differentiate a product, just differentiate both parts and multiply.\" Use an example to explain why this is incorrect.

Solution 15

The derivative of a product is not the product of the derivatives.

For example, if \(f(x)=x\sin x\), then

\[ f'(x)=\sin x+x\cos x \]

But multiplying the derivatives would give

\[ (1)(\cos x)=\cos x \]

Those are not the same, so the correct rule is \(u'v+uv'\).

Problem 16

Find the derivative of \(v(x)=\sin x(2x^2-5x+1)\).

Solution 16

Let \(u=\sin x\) and \(w=2x^2-5x+1\).

\[ u'=\cos x \quad \text{and} \quad w'=4x-5 \] \[ v'(x)=u'w+uw'=(\cos x)(2x^2-5x+1)+(\sin x)(4x-5) \] \[ v'(x)=\cos x(2x^2-5x+1)+\sin x(4x-5) \]
Problem 17

If \(f(x)=(x^2+3x)\cos x\), find \(f'(0)\).

Solution 17

Differentiate first.

\[ f'(x)=(2x+3)\cos x-(x^2+3x)\sin x \]

Now substitute \(x=0\).

\[ f'(0)=(2(0)+3)\cos 0-((0)^2+3(0))\sin 0=3 \]

So \(f'(0)=3\).

Problem 18

Find the derivative of \(w(x)=(x^4+1)\sin x\).

Solution 18

Differentiate the polynomial and trig factors, then apply the product rule.

\[ w'(x)=4x^3\sin x+(x^4+1)\cos x \]

This derivative keeps both product-rule terms.

Problem 19

If \(g(x)=(x^2+1)\sin x\), find \(g'(\tfrac{\pi}{2})\).

Solution 19

First find the derivative.

\[ g'(x)=2x\sin x+(x^2+1)\cos x \]

Now substitute \(x=\tfrac{\pi}{2}\).

\[ g'\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=2\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+\left(\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^2+1\right)\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) \] \[ g'\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=\pi+0=\pi \]
Problem 20

Find the derivative of \(y=(x^3-2x)\sin x\), then explain which part came from differentiating the polynomial factor and which part came from differentiating the trig factor.

Solution 20

Use the product rule with \(u=x^3-2x\) and \(v=\sin x\).

\[ u'=3x^2-2 \quad \text{and} \quad v'=\cos x \] \[ y'=(3x^2-2)\sin x+(x^3-2x)\cos x \]

The term \((3x^2-2)\sin x\) comes from differentiating the polynomial factor.

The term \((x^3-2x)\cos x\) comes from differentiating the trig factor.
