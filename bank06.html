Module 6 Master Question Bank

Section 6.A — Graph Quadratics & Vertex Structure

Objective A: I can graph quadratic equations by identifying the vertex, axis of symmetry, intercepts, and direction of opening.

1. Find the vertex of $f(x) = -2x^2 + 8x - 6$.

2. Convert $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5$ to vertex form.

3. Determine whether $f(x) = 3(x - 2)^2 + 1$ has a maximum or minimum. State the value.

4. Identify the axis of symmetry of $f(x) = 2x^2 - 4x + 1$.

5. Graph $f(x) = (x + 1)^2 - 4$. Identify the vertex and intercepts.

6. Find the x-intercepts of $f(x) = x^2 - 9$.

7. Complete the square for $f(x) = x^2 + 4x + 1$.

8. Determine the range of $f(x) = -x^2 + 6x - 5$.

9. Compare $f(x) = x^2$ and $g(x) = 4x^2$. Which is narrower? Explain.

10. Given a parabola with vertex $(3, -2)$ and opening upward, write its equation in vertex form.

11. A quadratic has axis of symmetry $x = -1$ and passes through $(0, 4)$. Write one possible equation.

Section 6.B — Factored Form & Multiplicity

Objective B: I can analyze and sketch polynomial functions written in factored form by identifying roots, multiplicity, and end behavior.

1. Describe the behavior of $f(x) = (x - 1)^2(x + 3)$ at each root.

2. Determine the degree and leading coefficient of $f(x) = -2(x - 4)^3(x + 1)$.

3. Sketch the end behavior of $f(x) = -x^4(x - 2)$.

4. Identify multiplicity and crossing behavior of $f(x) = (x + 2)^4$.

5. Determine whether both ends go up, down, or opposite for $f(x) = -3x^5 + 2x$.

6. Given roots $-2$ (multiplicity 2) and $1$ (multiplicity 1), what is the minimum possible degree?

7. Analyze the end behavior of $f(x) = 4(x - 3)^2(x + 1)^2$.

8. Explain how multiplicity affects the shape of a graph near a root.

9. Given factored form $f(x) = (x - 2)(x + 3)(x - 5)$, how many turning points are possible?

10. Construct a degree 4 polynomial with roots $1$ (multiplicity 2) and $-2$ (multiplicity 2).

11. A polynomial crosses the x-axis at $x = -3, 2$ and bounces at $x = 1$. What is the minimum possible degree?

Section 6.C — Writing Equations from Structure

Objective C: I can construct polynomial equations from given intercepts, multiplicities, and additional information such as a known point.

1. Write the factored form of a polynomial with roots $x = -1$ and $x = 4$.

2. A graph crosses at $x = -3$ and touches at $x = 2$. Write a possible equation.

3. A quadratic has x-intercepts at $1$ and $5$ and passes through $(0, -10)$. Find the equation.

4. Construct a degree 3 polynomial with roots $0, 2,$ and $-2$.

5. Given roots $1$ (multiplicity 2) and $-4$, write the general form with unknown $a$.

6. Find a polynomial with roots $-2, 0,$ and $3$ and leading coefficient $2$.

7. Write an equation if a polynomial touches at $x = 1$ and crosses at $x = -2$.

8. Determine the leading coefficient if roots are $2$ and $-3$ and the y-intercept is $12$.

9. Explain why multiple equations can share the same intercepts.

10. Construct a degree 4 polynomial with positive leading coefficient and roots $-1$ (mult 2) and $3$ (mult 2).

11. A polynomial crosses at $x = -2, 4$ and has y-intercept $-8$. Find one possible equation.

Section 6.D — Complete Polynomial Analysis

Objective D: I can analyze a polynomial function completely by identifying all key features and interpreting its behavior.

1. For $f(x) = 2x^4 - 8x^2$, identify: degree, leading coefficient, roots, multiplicities, end behavior, symmetry, domain, and range.

2. For $f(x) = -x^3 + 6x$, identify: degree, leading coefficient, roots, multiplicities, end behavior, symmetry, domain, and range.

3. For $f(x) = (x - 1)^2(x + 3)$, identify: degree, roots, multiplicities, end behavior, and maximum possible turning points.

4. For $f(x) = -4x^6 + 2x^3 - 1$, identify: degree, leading coefficient, end behavior, and maximum turning points.

5. Determine whether $f(x) = x^4 - 3x^2$ is even, odd, or neither. Also identify its degree and end behavior.

6. For $f(x) = (x - 2)^2(x^2 - 9)$, identify: degree, roots, multiplicities, end behavior, and maximum turning points.

7. A polynomial has roots $-3, 1,$ and $4$ and negative leading coefficient. Identify its degree (minimum possible), multiplicities, and end behavior.

8. For $f(x) = x^4 + 2x^2$, identify: degree, symmetry, end behavior, domain, and range.

9. A polynomial crosses at $x = -2, 1, 3$ and bounces at $x = 0$. Identify the minimum possible degree and end behavior.

10. For $f(x) = x^5 - 5x^3 + 4x$, identify: degree, roots, multiplicities, symmetry, end behavior, domain, and range.

11. For $f(x) = -2(x - 1)^2(x + 2)(x - 3)$, identify: degree, leading coefficient, roots, multiplicities, end behavior, and maximum turning points.

Section 6.E — Above & Beyond: Structured Accumulation

Objective E: I can evaluate and interpret sigma notation as structured accumulation.

1. Expand and evaluate: $\sum_{i=1}^{6} i^2$

2. Write the following sum using sigma notation: $3 + 6 + 9 + 12 + 15$

3. Expand and evaluate: $\sum_{i=0}^{4} (i + 2)$

4. Let $f(x) = 2x + 1$. Evaluate: $\sum_{i=1}^{4} f(i)$

5. Let $f(x) = x^2 - 1$. Evaluate: $\sum_{i=1}^{3} f(i)$

6. Simplify and write as a formula in terms of $n$: $\sum_{k=1}^{n} 3k$

7. Separate and simplify: $\sum_{k=1}^{4} (2k + 3)$

8. Let $f(x) = x^2 - 3x$. Evaluate: $\sum_{i=0}^{5} f(2i)$

9. Rewrite: $\sum_{i=1}^{4} (i^2 + 2i)$ as two separate summations.

10. Explain why constants can be factored out of a summation.

11. In your own words, explain what the bounds in $\sum_{i=2}^{6} f(i)$ tell you about the total being calculated.
