SECTION 8.B

Operations with Rational Expressions

Rational expressions extend fraction arithmetic into algebra. The rules remain the same — but factoring now reveals the structure that makes simplification possible.
“You cannot simplify what you cannot see. Factoring reveals what can cancel.”

Learning Objectives

  • I can simplify rational expressions by factoring and canceling common factors.
  • I can identify and state all restrictions on the variable.
  • I can multiply and divide rational expressions using structural reasoning.
  • I can add and subtract rational expressions using common denominators.
  • I can justify why canceling terms instead of factors produces incorrect results.

Introduction: Algebraic Fractions

A rational expression is a fraction whose numerator and denominator are polynomials. The same arithmetic rules used for numerical fractions apply:

  • Multiply straight across.
  • Divide by multiplying by the reciprocal.
  • Use common denominators to add or subtract.

However, rational expressions require one additional skill: factoring. Without factoring, common factors remain hidden and simplification becomes impossible.

For example,

$$ \frac{x^2 - 16}{x - 4} $$

cannot be simplified until the numerator is factored.

Structural Rule: Only factors may cancel — never individual terms.

Domain and Restrictions

Unlike numerical fractions, rational expressions depend on variables. A denominator may not equal zero.

Any value that makes the denominator zero must be excluded from the domain. These excluded values are called restrictions.

Even if a factor cancels, the restriction remains because it existed in the original expression.

Key Vocabulary

Rational Expression: A quotient of two polynomials where the denominator is not zero.
Restriction: A value of the variable that makes the denominator equal to zero.
Equivalent Rational Expressions: Two expressions that produce the same value for all permissible inputs.
Reciprocal: The multiplicative inverse of a fraction; used when dividing rational expressions.

Operational Strategy

  1. Factor all numerators and denominators completely.
  2. State restrictions before canceling.
  3. Cancel only common factors.
  4. Multiply straight across when multiplying.
  5. Rewrite division as multiplication by the reciprocal.
  6. Find a common denominator before adding or subtracting.

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Simplify and State Restrictions

Simplify:

$$ \frac{x^2 - 25}{x^2 - 5x} $$

Step 1: Identify restrictions.

$$ x^2 - 5x = x(x - 5) $$ Restrictions: $$ x \ne 0, \quad x \ne 5 $$

Step 2: Factor numerator and denominator.

$$ \frac{(x - 5)(x + 5)}{x(x - 5)} $$

Step 3: Cancel common factors.

$$ \frac{x + 5}{x} $$

Final Answer:

$$ \frac{x + 5}{x}, \quad x \ne 0,\,5 $$

Even though $(x - 5)$ cancels, $x = 5$ remains excluded.

Example 2 — Multiply Rational Expressions

Multiply:

$$ \frac{2x}{x - 3} \cdot \frac{x^2 - 9}{4x} $$

Step 1: Identify restrictions.

$$ x \ne 3, \quad x \ne 0 $$

Step 2: Factor completely.

$$ \frac{2x}{x - 3} \cdot \frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{4x} $$

Step 3: Cancel common factors.

Cancel $(x - 3)$ and $x$: $$ \frac{2(x + 3)}{4} $$

Step 4: Simplify numerical factor.

$$ \frac{x + 3}{2} $$

Final Answer:

$$ \frac{x + 3}{2}, \quad x \ne 0,\,3 $$

Example 3 — Divide Rational Expressions

Divide:

$$ \frac{x^2 - 4x}{x^2 - 16} \div \frac{x}{x - 4} $$

Step 1: Rewrite division as multiplication by the reciprocal.

$$ \frac{x^2 - 4x}{x^2 - 16} \cdot \frac{x - 4}{x} $$

Step 2: Factor completely.

$$ \frac{x(x - 4)}{(x - 4)(x + 4)} \cdot \frac{x - 4}{x} $$

Step 3: Identify restrictions.

$$ x \ne 0, \quad x \ne 4, \quad x \ne -4 $$

Step 4: Cancel common factors.

Cancel $x$ and one $(x - 4)$: $$ \frac{x - 4}{x + 4} $$

Final Answer:

$$ \frac{x - 4}{x + 4}, \quad x \ne 0,\,4,\,-4 $$

Example 4 — Add Rational Expressions (Different Denominators)

Add:

$$ \frac{3}{x} + \frac{2}{x - 5} $$

Step 1: Identify restrictions.

$$ x \ne 0, \quad x \ne 5 $$

Step 2: Find a common denominator.

Common denominator: $$ x(x - 5) $$

Step 3: Rewrite each fraction.

$$ \frac{3(x - 5)}{x(x - 5)} + \frac{2x}{x(x - 5)} $$

Step 4: Combine numerators.

$$ \frac{3x - 15 + 2x}{x(x - 5)} = \frac{5x - 15}{x(x - 5)} $$

Step 5: Factor numerator.

$$ \frac{5(x - 3)}{x(x - 5)} $$

No common factors remain.

Final Answer:

$$ \frac{5(x - 3)}{x(x - 5)}, \quad x \ne 0,\,5 $$

Example 5 — Subtract Rational Expressions (Factoring Required)

Subtract:

$$ \frac{x}{x^2 - 9} - \frac{1}{x - 3} $$

Step 1: Factor denominators.

$$ x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3) $$ Restrictions: $$ x \ne 3, \quad x \ne -3 $$

Step 2: Rewrite with common denominator.

$$ \frac{x}{(x - 3)(x + 3)} - \frac{x + 3}{(x - 3)(x + 3)} $$

Step 3: Combine numerators.

$$ \frac{x - (x + 3)}{(x - 3)(x + 3)} = \frac{-3}{(x - 3)(x + 3)} $$

Final Answer:

$$ \frac{-3}{(x - 3)(x + 3)}, \quad x \ne 3,\,-3 $$

Notice how factoring exposed the necessary common denominator.

Common Mistakes

  • Canceling Terms Instead of Factors:
    In an expression like $$ \frac{x + 3}{x} $$ the $x$ in the numerator cannot cancel with the denominator because it is not a common factor. Only entire factors may cancel.
  • Skipping Factoring:
    Many rational expressions appear unsimplifiable until they are factored. Without factoring first, common factors remain hidden.
  • Forgetting Domain Restrictions:
    Even if a factor cancels, its restriction remains. The domain is determined by the original denominator, not the simplified result.
  • Not Rewriting Division Properly:
    Division must always be rewritten as multiplication by the reciprocal. Forgetting to flip the second fraction produces incorrect results.
  • Combining Numerators Without a Common Denominator:
    You cannot add: $$ \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{x - 1} $$ by adding numerators directly. A common denominator must be established first.
  • Arithmetic Errors After Structural Steps:
    Even when structure is correct, careless algebra (especially sign errors) leads to incorrect final expressions.

Summary of Rational Expression Operations

Operation Structural Requirement Key Reminder
Simplifying Factor numerator and denominator completely. Cancel only common factors. State restrictions first.
Multiplying Factor all parts before multiplying. Cancel across numerators and denominators before multiplying straight across.
Dividing Rewrite division as multiplication by the reciprocal. Flip the second fraction first, then factor and cancel.
Adding / Subtracting Find a common denominator. Combine only numerators after denominators match.
Domain Denominator cannot equal zero. Restrictions remain even if factors cancel.

Core Structural Principles:
  • Factoring reveals what can cancel.
  • Canceling is multiplication-based, not addition-based.
  • Restrictions come from the original expression.
  • Structure first, arithmetic second.
  • Every operation follows fraction logic — with algebraic structure exposed.

Solutions

Level 1 — Simplify and State Restrictions

1. $$ \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} = \frac{(x - 1)(x + 1)}{x - 1} = x + 1 \quad (x \ne 1) $$

2. $$ \frac{3x^2 - 12x}{6x} = \frac{3x(x - 4)}{6x} = \frac{x - 4}{2} \quad (x \ne 0) $$

3. $$ \frac{x^2 - 36}{x^2 - 6x} = \frac{(x - 6)(x + 6)}{x(x - 6)} = \frac{x + 6}{x} \quad (x \ne 0,\,6) $$

4. $$ \frac{5x}{10x - 20} = \frac{5x}{10(x - 2)} = \frac{x}{2(x - 2)} \quad (x \ne 2) $$

Level 2 — Multiply and Divide

5. $$ \frac{x - 4}{x} \cdot \frac{(x - 4)(x + 4)}{2} = \frac{(x - 4)^2(x + 4)}{2x} \quad (x \ne 0) $$

6. $$ \frac{2x}{(x - 3)(x + 3)} \cdot \frac{x - 3}{x} = \frac{2}{x + 3} \quad (x \ne 0,\,\pm3) $$

7. Rewrite division: $$ \frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} \cdot \frac{x + 2}{x - 3} = \frac{x + 3}{x - 2} \quad (x \ne \pm2,\,3) $$

8. Rewrite division: $$ \frac{x(x - 5)}{x} \cdot \frac{x^2}{x - 5} = x^2 \quad (x \ne 0,\,5) $$

Level 3 — Add and Subtract

9. Common denominator $x(x - 2)$: $$ \frac{4(x - 2) + x}{x(x - 2)} = \frac{5x - 8}{x(x - 2)} \quad (x \ne 0,\,2) $$

10. Common denominator $(x + 3)(x - 3)$: $$ \frac{2(x - 3) - 5(x + 3)}{(x + 3)(x - 3)} = \frac{-3x - 21}{x^2 - 9} \quad (x \ne \pm3) $$

11. Common denominator $x(x - 1)$: $$ \frac{x^2 + (x - 1)}{x(x - 1)} = \frac{x^2 + x - 1}{x(x - 1)} \quad (x \ne 0,\,1) $$

12. Common denominator $(x - 2)(x + 2)$: $$ \frac{x + 2}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} + \frac{2(x + 2)}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} $$ $$ = \frac{3x + 4}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} \quad (x \ne \pm2) $$

Level 4 — Multi-Step Structure

13. $$ \frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} \cdot \frac{x + 2}{x - 3} = \frac{x + 3}{x - 2} \quad (x \ne 2,\,-2,\,3) $$

14. $$ \frac{(x - 4)(x + 4)}{(x - 4)^2} = \frac{x + 4}{x - 4} \quad (x \ne 4) $$

15. $$ \frac{x + 5}{x} + \frac{1}{x} = \frac{x + 6}{x} \quad (x \ne 0,\,5) $$

Level 5 — Justify

16. The $x$ in $x + 2$ is part of a sum, not a factor. Only entire factors multiplied together may cancel.

17. Factoring reveals multiplicative structure. Without factoring, you cannot identify true common factors.

18. A removable discontinuity occurs when a common factor cancels from numerator and denominator. The function simplifies, but the original denominator restriction remains, creating a hole in the graph.
