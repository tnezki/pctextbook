Problem 1

Solve and check for extraneous solutions: $$\log(x-1)+\log(x-4)=\log 12$$

Solution 1

Domain restrictions: \(x-1>0\) and \(x-4>0\), so \(x>4\).

Combine the logarithms:

$$\log[(x-1)(x-4)] = \log 12$$

Set the arguments equal:

$$ (x-1)(x-4)=12 $$ $$ x^2-5x+4=12 $$ $$ x^2-5x-8=0 $$

Use the quadratic formula:

$$ x=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{25+32}}{2}=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{57}}{2} $$

Check both solutions against \(x>4\):

  • \(x=\frac{5+\sqrt{57}}{2}\) is greater than 4, so it is valid.
  • \(x=\frac{5-\sqrt{57}}{2}\) is not greater than 4, so it is extraneous.

Solution: $$x=\frac{5+\sqrt{57}}{2}$$

Problem 3

Solve and check for extraneous solutions: $$\log(x-3)-\log(x-7)=\log 2$$

Solution 3

Domain restrictions: \(x-3>0\) and \(x-7>0\), so \(x>7\).

Use the quotient property:

$$ \log\left(\frac{x-3}{x-7}\right)=\log 2 $$

Set the arguments equal:

$$ \frac{x-3}{x-7}=2 $$ $$ x-3=2x-14 $$ $$ 11=x $$

Check:

$$ \log(8)-\log(4)=\log 2 $$

This is true, and \(11>7\).

Solution: $$x=11$$

Problem 4

Solve and check for extraneous solutions: $$\ln(x+4)-\ln(x-1)=\ln 5$$

Solution 4

Domain restrictions: \(x+4>0\) and \(x-1>0\), so \(x>1\).

Use the quotient property:

$$ \ln\left(\frac{x+4}{x-1}\right)=\ln 5 $$

Set the arguments equal:

$$ \frac{x+4}{x-1}=5 $$ $$ x+4=5x-5 $$ $$ 9=4x $$ $$ x=\frac{9}{4} $$

Check: \(\frac{9}{4}>1\), so the value is in the domain and satisfies the equation.

Solution: $$x=\frac{9}{4}$$

Problem 6

Solve and check for extraneous solutions: $$\ln(x^2-9)=2$$

Solution 6

Domain restriction: \(x^2-9>0\), so \(|x|>3\).

Rewrite in exponential form:

$$ x^2-9=e^2 $$ $$ x^2=e^2+9 $$ $$ x=\pm \sqrt{e^2+9} $$

Check the domain: both values satisfy \(|x|>3\), so both are valid.

Solutions: $$x=\pm\sqrt{e^2+9}$$
