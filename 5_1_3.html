5.1.3 What’s up with e?

The Number e
5-28

A common application of the number e is in the formula for continuously compounded interest. The A (amount) you will have after t years if P (principal) dollars are invested at r annual interest, compounded continuously, is A = Pert.

In countries where the rate of inflation is extremely high, the average annual inflation rate often reaches rates that rise very fast. However, some countries have astronomical inflation rates. For example, the official inflation rate for Zimbabwe in 2007 was 2200%.

  1. Assuming continuous compounding at the rate of 2200% inflation, how much will a 1500 ZWD (ZWD = Zimbabwean dollar) bag of groceries cost in just one month? (Be careful! What is the corresponding r-value?)

  2. In order to keep pace, banks in countries like Zimbabwe offer interest rates that sound too good to be true! A bank might actually offer 100% interest on a deposit. How much money will you have after one year if you deposit $500 at 100% interest, compounded monthly?

  3. How much money will you have after one year if you deposit $500 at 100% interest, compounded continuously?

  4. When will you have $2400 if you deposit $500 at 100% interest, compounded continuously? Approximate your answer using a graphing calculator or with a method of your choice.
