Level 1 — Basic Properties

Directions: Expand each logarithmic expression.

  1. \( \log(uv) \)
  2. \( \log\left(\frac{m}{n}\right) \)
  3. \( \log(x^6) \)
  4. \( \log(7y) \)

Condense

Directions: Condense each expression into a single logarithm.

  1. \( \log p + \log q \)
  2. \( \log a - \log b \)
  3. \( 3\log x \)
  4. \( \log r + \log s \)

Identify the Property

Directions: Identify the logarithm property used.

  1. \( \log(ab)=\log a + \log b \)
  2. \( \log\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)=\log a - \log b \)
  3. \( \log(x^3)=3\log x \)
  4. \( 4\log x = \log(x^4) \)

Level 5 — Conceptual & Justification

  1. The quotient rule comes from exponent rules where division subtracts exponents.
  2. Exponents become coefficients when brought in front of the logarithm.
  3. Addition inside a log does not correspond to multiplication, so it cannot be separated.
  4. Logarithms undo exponent operations like multiplication and powers.
  5. Rewriting helps apply properties correctly, especially with roots and powers.
