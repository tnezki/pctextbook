Warm-Up

Use the questions below to bring forward the product-rule work and line-writing skills that support tangent-line problems.

1. Find the derivative of $f(x)=(x+1)\sin x$.

Answer: $f'(x)=\sin x+(x+1)\cos x$.

2. Evaluate the derivative from Question 1 at $x=0$.

Answer: $f'(0)=1$.

3. Find the point on the graph of $f(x)=(x+1)\sin x$ when $x=0$.

Answer: $f(0)=0$, so the point is $(0,0)$.

4. Write the equation of the line through $(0,0)$ with slope $1$.

Answer: $y=x$.

Intro

Directions

Read the introduction aloud with a partner or in a small group. As you read, annotate the text by marking important ideas, circling key vocabulary, and writing short notes about connections, questions, or predictions in the margins.

In the previous section, the derivative of a product gave a new way to compute how a function changes. This section takes the next step by using that derivative to build a tangent line. The slope of a tangent line is still the derivative at a point, but now that idea becomes part of a larger task: find the slope, find the point on the graph, and write the equation of the line that best matches the function at that instant.

This idea connects naturally to science and engineering because tangent lines are a way of describing local behavior. In physics, a derivative can represent velocity, and the tangent line shows how position is changing at an instant. In economics or biology, a tangent line can show how a quantity is increasing or decreasing at a given moment. The line itself becomes a visual model of rate of change.

Tangent lines also matter because they connect algebra, geometry, and function behavior in one picture. A function gives the curve, the derivative gives the slope, and the tangent line shows what the function is doing right there. For product functions such as $(x^2+1)\sin x$ or $\cos x(x^2-1)$, that means a single tangent-line problem combines algebraic structure, trigonometric behavior, and a geometric interpretation of change.

Historically, tangent lines were one of the driving questions behind the development of calculus. Mathematicians wanted a precise way to describe instantaneous direction and change on a curve. The derivative answered that question by turning local behavior into slope. Once that connection was made, tangent lines became one of the clearest geometric interpretations of differentiation.

Notation Reminder

Common Tangent-Line Notation

  • $m=f'(a)$ gives the slope of the tangent line at $x=a$.
  • The point on the graph is $(a,f(a))$.
  • Point-slope form: $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$

In a tangent-line problem, the derivative gives the slope, the original function gives the point, and point-slope form builds the equation of the line.

From Derivative to Tangent-Line Slope

The first step in a tangent-line problem is to find the slope. That slope comes from the derivative, not from the original function value. When the function is a product, the product rule is used first, and only after that do we evaluate at the given $x$ value.

Slope of the Tangent Line

\[ m=f'(a) \]

The slope at $x=a$ comes from evaluating the derivative at that same input value.

Example 1

For $f(x)=(x+1)\sin x$, find the slope of the tangent line at $x=0$.

Answer:

\[ f'(x)=\sin x+(x+1)\cos x \] \[ f'(0)=\sin 0+(0+1)\cos 0=1 \]

The slope of the tangent line is $1$.

You Try It 1

For $g(x)=\cos x(x^2-1)$, find the slope of the tangent line at $x=0$.

Answer:

\[ g'(x)=-\sin x(x^2-1)+\cos x(2x) \] \[ g'(0)=0 \]

The slope of the tangent line is $0$.

Stop and Discuss

Why does the slope in a tangent-line problem come from $f'(a)$ instead of from $f(a)$?

Answer: $f'(a)$ measures the rate of change and gives the slope of the tangent line. The value $f(a)$ gives the point on the graph, not the slope.

Finding the Point on the Graph

After finding the slope, the next step is to find the point where the tangent line touches the graph. That point comes from the original function, not from the derivative. If the tangent line is at $x=a$, then the point is $(a,f(a))$.

Point on the Curve

\[ (a,f(a)) \]

Use the original function value to locate the point of tangency.

Example 2

For $h(x)=(x^2+1)\sin x$, find the point on the graph when $x=0$.

Answer:

\[ h(0)=((0)^2+1)\sin 0=0 \]

The point on the graph is $(0,0)$.

You Try It 2

For $p(x)=\cos x(x+2)$, find the point on the graph when $x=0$.

Answer:

\[ p(0)=\cos 0(0+2)=2 \]

The point on the graph is $(0,2)$.

Stop and Discuss

Why do tangent-line problems need information from both the derivative and the original function?

Answer: The derivative gives the slope, and the original function gives the point on the graph. Both are needed to write the equation of the tangent line.

Writing the Equation of the Tangent Line

Once the slope and the point are known, the tangent line can be written using point-slope form. This step turns the derivative information into a full linear equation that describes the function locally.

Point-Slope Form

\[ y-y_1=m(x-x_1) \]

Substitute the slope and the point of tangency into the formula.

Example 3

Find the equation of the tangent line to $q(x)=(x^2+1)\sin x$ at $x=0$.

Answer: First find the slope.

\[ q'(x)=2x\sin x+(x^2+1)\cos x \] \[ q'(0)=1 \]

Now find the point.

\[ q(0)=0 \]

The point is $(0,0)$ and the slope is $1$.

\[ y-0=1(x-0) \] \[ y=x \]

You Try It 3

Find the equation of the tangent line to $r(x)=\cos x(x+2)$ at $x=0$.

Answer:

\[ r'(x)=-\sin x(x+2)+\cos x \] \[ r'(0)=1 \] \[ r(0)=2 \]

The point is $(0,2)$ and the slope is $1$.

\[ y-2=1(x-0) \] \[ y=x+2 \]

Stop and Discuss

How does point-slope form connect the derivative to the graph of the function?

Answer: The derivative provides the slope, the original function provides the point, and point-slope form turns those two pieces into the equation of the tangent line on the graph.

Connecting the Tangent Line to Rate of Change

A tangent line is not only a geometric object. Its slope tells how the function is changing at that exact point. A positive slope means the function is increasing there, a negative slope means it is decreasing there, and a slope of zero means the function is momentarily flat at that point.

Interpreting the tangent line helps connect symbolic work back to meaning. In this way, the tangent line becomes a visible picture of the instantaneous rate of change described by the derivative.

Example 4

Find the tangent line to $s(x)=\sin x(x^2+1)$ at $x=0$, and explain what the slope tells you about the function at that point.

Answer:

\[ s'(x)=\cos x(x^2+1)+\sin x(2x) \] \[ s'(0)=1 \] \[ s(0)=0 \] \[ y-0=1(x-0) \] \[ y=x \]

The slope is positive, so the function is increasing at a rate of $1$ unit of $y$ for each unit of $x$ at that instant.

You Try It 4

Find the tangent line to $t(x)=\cos x(x^2-1)$ at $x=0$, and explain what the slope tells you about the function at that point.

Answer:

\[ t'(x)=-\sin x(x^2-1)+\cos x(2x) \] \[ t'(0)=0 \] \[ t(0)=-1 \] \[ y+1=0(x-0) \] \[ y=-1 \]

The slope is $0$, so the function is momentarily flat there and the tangent line is horizontal.

Stop and Discuss

What does the slope of a tangent line tell you about the rate of change of the function at that point?

Answer: The slope of the tangent line tells the instantaneous rate of change of the function at that point. Positive slope means increasing, negative slope means decreasing, and zero slope means momentarily flat.

Summary Concepts

  • The slope of a tangent line at $x=a$ is $f'(a)$.
  • The point of tangency is found from the original function as $(a,f(a))$.
  • The equation of the tangent line can be written with point-slope form.
  • The product rule is used first when the function is a product.
  • The slope of the tangent line shows the instantaneous rate of change at that point.

Common Mistakes

  • Using $f(a)$ as the slope instead of $f'(a)$.
  • Using the derivative to find the point on the graph instead of the original function.
  • Forgetting point-slope form after finding the slope and point.
  • Forgetting product-rule structure or the negative sign in the derivative of cosine.

The slope is $1$.

Example 2 Solution

For $h(x)=(x^2+1)\sin x$, find the point on the graph when $x=0$.

\[ h(0)=((0)^2+1)\sin 0=0 \]

The point is $(0,0)$.

\[ q'(x)=2x\sin x+(x^2+1)\cos x \] \[ q'(0)=1 \] \[ q(0)=0 \] \[ y-0=1(x-0) \] \[ y=x \]
