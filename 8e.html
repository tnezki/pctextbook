SECTION 8.E

Limits and Discontinuities

A limit describes the value a function approaches as the input approaches a particular number. It focuses on behavior near a point — not necessarily the value at that point.
“Limits describe approach, not arrival.”

Learning Objectives

  • I can evaluate limits graphically using one-sided and two-sided notation.
  • I can evaluate limits algebraically using direct substitution and factoring.
  • I can distinguish between removable, infinite, and jump discontinuities.
  • I can correctly use limit notation and interpret behavior near undefined points.
  • I can connect rational expression structure to limit behavior.

Introduction: Behavior Near a Point

A limit answers the question:

What value does the function approach as $x$ approaches a specific number?

The function does not need to be defined at that number for a limit to exist.

Three outcomes are possible when evaluating limits:

  • The function approaches a single value → the limit exists.
  • The left and right approaches differ → the limit does not exist.
  • The function increases or decreases without bound → infinite behavior.

Key Vocabulary

Limit: The value a function approaches as the input approaches a specific number.
One-Sided Limit: A limit taken from the left ($x \to a^-$) or right ($x \to a^+$).
Two-Sided Limit: Exists only if both one-sided limits are equal.
Removable Discontinuity: A hole caused by a canceled factor.
Infinite Discontinuity: A vertical asymptote where function values increase or decrease without bound.
Jump Discontinuity: A break where left and right limits are not equal.

Core Strategy 1: Graphical Evaluation

When evaluating limits from a graph:

  1. Follow the curve as $x$ approaches the target value.
  2. Determine the left-hand limit.
  3. Determine the right-hand limit.
  4. If both sides match, the two-sided limit exists.
The function value at the point does not determine the limit.

Core Strategy 2: Algebraic Evaluation

When evaluating limits algebraically:

  1. Try direct substitution first.
  2. If substitution gives a number → that is the limit.
  3. If substitution gives $\frac{0}{0}$ → factor and simplify.
  4. If the denominator remains zero → analyze one-sided behavior.
An indeterminate form $\frac{0}{0}$ signals that simplification is required.

Classifying Discontinuities

  • If a factor cancels → Removable discontinuity (hole).
  • If a denominator factor remains → Infinite discontinuity (vertical asymptote).
  • If left and right limits differ → Jump discontinuity.

Factoring reveals which type of discontinuity is present.

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Direct Substitution

Evaluate:

$$ \lim_{x \to 3} (2x^2 - x + 1) $$

Step 1: Substitute directly.

$$ 2(3)^2 - 3 + 1 $$ $$ = 18 - 3 + 1 $$ $$ = 16 $$

Final Answer:

$$ 16 $$

Polynomials are continuous everywhere, so substitution always works.

Example 2 — Indeterminate Form (Factoring Required)

Evaluate:

$$ \lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2} $$

Step 1: Substitute directly.

$$ \frac{0}{0} $$

This is indeterminate.

Step 2: Factor numerator.

$$ \frac{(x - 2)(x + 2)}{x - 2} $$

Step 3: Cancel common factor.

$$ x + 2 $$

Step 4: Substitute again.

$$ 2 + 2 = 4 $$

Final Answer:

$$ 4 $$

This is a removable discontinuity (hole at $x = 2$).

Example 3 — Infinite Limit

Evaluate the one-sided limits:

$$ \lim_{x \to 2^-} \frac{1}{x - 2} \quad \lim_{x \to 2^+} \frac{1}{x - 2} $$

As $x \to 2^-$, the denominator is a small negative number.

$$ \frac{1}{\text{small negative}} \to -\infty $$

As $x \to 2^+$, the denominator is a small positive number.

$$ \frac{1}{\text{small positive}} \to +\infty $$

Final Answer:

$$ \lim_{x \to 2^-} = -\infty \quad \lim_{x \to 2^+} = +\infty $$

The two-sided limit does not exist.

This is an infinite discontinuity (vertical asymptote).

Example 4 — Limit Exists Even If Function Is Undefined

Suppose:

$$ f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2 - 1 & x \ne 1 \\ 5 & x = 1 \end{cases} $$ Evaluate: $$ \lim_{x \to 1} f(x) $$

The expression near $x = 1$ is:

$$ x^2 - 1 $$ Substitute: $$ 1^2 - 1 = 0 $$

Final Answer:

$$ 0 $$

The limit exists even though $f(1) = 5$.

Example 5 — Classification

Classify the discontinuity of:

$$ f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} $$
Factor numerator: $$ \frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{x - 3} $$ Cancel common factor: $$ x + 3 $$

There is a hole at $x = 3$.

Classification:

Removable discontinuity.

Common Mistakes

  • Confusing the Function Value with the Limit:
    The limit depends on what the function approaches, not necessarily the value of the function at that point.
  • Stopping After Getting $\frac{0}{0}$:
    An indeterminate form means additional algebraic simplification is required.
  • Canceling Terms Instead of Factors:
    Only entire factors may cancel. Factoring is often required before simplification.
  • Ignoring One-Sided Limits:
    If left and right limits are different, the two-sided limit does not exist.
  • Assuming Infinite Limits Exist:
    If a function approaches $\pm\infty$, the limit does not exist as a finite number.
  • Forgetting to Classify the Discontinuity:
    Every discontinuity should be identified as removable, infinite, or jump.

Summary of Limits and Discontinuities

Structural Situation What to Do Interpretation
Polynomial expression Use direct substitution. Polynomials are continuous everywhere.
Substitution gives $\frac{0}{0}$ Factor and simplify. Removable discontinuity (hole).
Denominator zero but no cancellation Analyze one-sided limits. Infinite discontinuity (vertical asymptote).
Left and right limits differ Compare one-sided limits. Jump discontinuity.
Function undefined at a point Check nearby behavior. Limit may still exist.

Core Structural Principles:
  • A limit describes approach, not arrival.
  • Always attempt substitution first.
  • $\frac{0}{0}$ signals factoring.
  • Vertical asymptotes create infinite limits.
  • Equal one-sided limits are required for a two-sided limit.
  • Discontinuities must be classified.

Level 1 — Direct Substitution

1. \[ \lim_{x \to 4} (x^2 - 3x + 2) \]

2. \[ \lim_{x \to -2} (5x^2 + x - 6) \]

3. \[ \lim_{x \to 1} (3x^3 - 2x + 4) \]

4. \[ \lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} \]

6. \[ \lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 - 3x + 2} \]

Level 3 — Infinite Limits

7. \[ \lim_{x \to 0^-} \frac{1}{x} \quad \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{1}{x} \]

8. \[ \lim_{x \to 5} \frac{1}{x - 5} \]

9. \[ \lim_{x \to -3} \frac{2}{x + 3} \]

Level 4 — Classification

10. Classify the discontinuity: \[ f(x) = \frac{x^2 - 16}{x - 4} \]

11. Classify the discontinuity: \[ f(x) = \frac{3}{x + 2} \]

12. Classify the discontinuity: \[ f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2 & x<1 \\ 3 & x \ge 1 \end{cases} \]

Level 5 — Justify

13. Explain why a removable discontinuity can have a limit even if the function is undefined at that point.

14. Explain why an infinite discontinuity prevents a finite two-sided limit from existing.

15. Explain why a jump discontinuity prevents the existence of a two-sided limit.

16. Explain why factoring is necessary when substitution produces $\frac{0}{0}$.

17. Describe how limits connect rational expressions to discontinuities.

18. Explain how limits prepare students for derivative concepts in calculus.

Solutions

Level 1 — Direct Substitution

1. \[ 4^2 - 3(4) + 2 = 16 - 12 + 2 = 6 \]

2. \[ 5(-2)^2 + (-2) - 6 = 5(4) - 2 - 6 = 20 - 8 = 12 \]

3. \[ 3(1)^3 - 2(1) + 4 = 3 - 2 + 4 = 5 \]

Level 2 — Indeterminate Forms

4. \[ \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} = \frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3} = x+3 \] Substitute: \[ 6 \]

5. \[ \frac{x^2 - 1}{x + 1} = \frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x+1} = x-1 \] Substitute: \[ -2 \]

6. \[ \frac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 - 3x + 2} = \frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{(x-1)(x-2)} = \frac{x+2}{x-1} \] Substitute: \[ \frac{4}{1} = 4 \]

Level 3 — Infinite Limits

7. \[ \lim_{x \to 0^-} \frac{1}{x} = -\infty \quad \lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{1}{x} = +\infty \] Two-sided limit does not exist.

8. \[ \lim_{x \to 5^-} \frac{1}{x-5} = -\infty \quad \lim_{x \to 5^+} \frac{1}{x-5} = +\infty \] Limit does not exist.

9. \[ \lim_{x \to -3^-} \frac{2}{x+3} = -\infty \quad \lim_{x \to -3^+} \frac{2}{x+3} = +\infty \] Limit does not exist.

Level 4 — Classification

10. \[ \frac{x^2 - 16}{x - 4} = \frac{(x-4)(x+4)}{x-4} = x+4 \] Removable discontinuity (hole at x = 4).

11. \[ \frac{3}{x+2} \] Denominator zero at x = -2 and does not cancel. Infinite discontinuity (vertical asymptote).

12. Left limit at x=1: \[ 1^2 = 1 \] Right limit: \[ 3 \] Since they differ → Jump discontinuity.

Level 5 — Justify

13. A removable discontinuity results from a canceled factor. The surrounding values still approach a single number, so the limit exists even if the function is undefined at that point.

14. Infinite discontinuities cause values to increase or decrease without bound. Since the function does not approach a finite value, the two-sided limit does not exist.

15. Jump discontinuities produce unequal left and right limits. A two-sided limit requires equality, so it does not exist.

16. Factoring removes common factors that create indeterminate forms. Without factoring, substitution cannot resolve the limit.

17. Rational expressions create discontinuities when denominators equal zero. Factoring reveals whether the discontinuity is removable or infinite.

18. Limits describe behavior as inputs approach a value. Derivatives use limits to define instantaneous rates of change, building directly on this concept.
