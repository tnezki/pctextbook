Module 01: Foundational Skills
Essential Standard
Students will recognize and evaluate functions using function notation, simplify expressions involving exponents and radicals, apply basic trigonometry to right triangles using special angles and the six trigonometric ratios, and solve real-world problems involving angles, sides, and forces in triangles using trigonometric relationships.
Section 1.A: Recognize Functions and Evaluate Expressions Given in Function Notation
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Determine whether a set of ordered pairs represents a function by applying the definition of a function and the vertical line test.
•  Identify functions given by equations by reasoning about whether each input produces exactly one output.
•  Evaluate function expressions at Level 1 by substituting given values into function notation and simplifying.
Section 1.B: Simplify Expressions with Exponents and Radicals
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Rewrite exponential expressions by applying the product, quotient, power, zero, and negative exponent rules.
•  Evaluate basic exponential functions by substituting values and applying properties of exponents to simplify.
•  Plug values into exponential formulas and evaluate, attending to precision when working with growth and decay models.
Section 1.C: Apply Basic Trigonometry to Right Triangles
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Find exact side lengths in special right triangles (30-60-90 and 45-45-90) and express answers in radical form.
•  Evaluate sine, cosine, and tangent for angles not on the unit circle using right triangle ratios.
•  Determine exact values of trigonometric functions for special angles using structure and repeated reasoning.
Section 1.D: Apply Trigonometry to Real World Problems
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Find unknown angles in a right triangle using inverse trigonometric functions and attend to precision in degree measure.
•  Find unknown sides in a right triangle by selecting and applying the appropriate trigonometric ratio.
•  Solve real-world force problems by applying trigonometry to decompose vectors and analyze triangle relationships.
Above and Beyond: Extended Learning (Section 1.E)
Learning Objectives for Above and Beyond – I can…
•  Find the average rate of change of a function from a table of values by calculating the ratio of change in output to change in input over a specified interval.
•  Find the average rate of change of a function from an equation by evaluating the function at the endpoints of a given interval and applying the rate of change formula.
•  Find the average rate of change from a graph by identifying output values at two points and reasoning about the slope of the secant line over the interval.
•  Interpret the average rate of change in context by attaching appropriate units and explaining the meaning of the rate in the situation.

Module 02: Solving and Systems
Essential Standard
Students will write equations of lines from given information in multiple forms, solve multi-step linear and quadratic equations including equations with variables in the denominator, solve linear systems using substitution and elimination, solve nonlinear systems involving lines and parabolas, and extend learning by writing and simplifying the difference quotient to express average rate of change from x to x+h.
Section 2.A: Write the Equation of a Line from Given Information
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Write the equation of a line in slope-intercept form and point-slope form, preferring y = a(x − h) + k, given slope and a point or two points.
•  Write the equation of a line from a table of values, a graph, or two coordinate points by identifying slope and using point-slope form.
•  Write and interpret the equation of a line in real-life situations, modeling the relationship between quantities and explaining slope and intercept in context.
Section 2.B: Solve Linear and Quadratic Equations
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Solve multi-step linear equations with variables on both sides and the distributive property, attending to precision in each step.
•  Solve quadratic equations by factoring to find rational solutions, including cases requiring rearrangement into standard form.
•  Solve equations with variables in the denominator of the form 3/x = ... that result in linear or quadratic equations, checking for extraneous solutions.
Section 2.C: Solve Linear Systems of Equations
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Solve a system of linear equations using substitution by isolating a variable and substituting into the second equation.
•  Solve a system of linear equations using elimination by adding or subtracting equations to eliminate a variable.
•  Interpret solutions to linear systems graphically by identifying the point of intersection and explaining what it means in context.
Section 2.D: Solve Nonlinear Systems of Equations
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Solve a system consisting of a line and a parabola algebraically and verify solutions graphically.
•  Solve a system of two parabolas by setting the equations equal and solving the resulting equation.
•  Interpret solutions to nonlinear systems graphically by identifying intersection points and reasoning about the number of solutions.
Above and Beyond: Extended Learning (Section 2.E)
Learning Objectives for Above and Beyond – I can…
•  Simplify the difference quotient for a given function by substituting x+h, expanding, and simplifying the expression.
•  Evaluate function expressions at Level 2 by substituting complex expressions including binomials into function notation.
•  Evaluate function expressions at Level 3, including compositions and multi-step substitutions, reasoning abstractly throughout.
•  Interpret secant line limits graphically to connect the difference quotient to the concept of instantaneous rate of change.

Module 03: Function Operations, Inverses, and Piecewise Functions
Essential Standard
Students will perform operations with functions including composition, write and interpret inverse functions algebraically and graphically, graph and evaluate piecewise-defined functions, and apply graphing form y = a(b(x − h))^n + k to transform parent functions including linear, quadratic, cubic, radical, cube root, absolute value, and reciprocal functions.
Section 3.A: Complete Operations with Functions, Including Composition
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Evaluate and simplify compositions of functions by substituting one function into another and simplifying the result.
•  Find and simplify the composition of functions written in terms of x, including (f ° g)(x) and (g ° f)(x), attending to order.
•  Perform and interpret function operations and compositions from mixed representations including equations, tables, and graphs.
Section 3.B: Write the Equation of the Inverse of a Function or Determine if Two Functions Are Inverses
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Find the inverse of a linear function by switching x and y and solving for y, and verify using composition.
•  Find the inverse of multi-step functions by applying inverse operations in reverse order and simplifying.
•  Determine whether two functions are inverses graphically by identifying reflection symmetry over the line y = x.
Section 3.C: Graph and/or Evaluate Piecewise-Defined Functions
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Evaluate piecewise-defined functions by identifying the correct piece for a given input value and substituting.
•  Graph piecewise-defined functions at Level 1 by graphing each piece over its restricted domain with correct endpoints.
•  Write the equation of a piecewise-defined function from a graph at Level 1 by identifying each piece’s rule and domain restriction.
Section 3.D: Transform Functions
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Apply graphing form y = a(b(x − h))^n + k to graph transformations of parent functions including linear, quadratic, cubic, radical, cube root, absolute value, and reciprocal functions.
•  Identify and describe the effect of stretch and compression parameters on the shape of a function’s graph.
•  Graph transformations of non-standard function shapes, including W-type and other non-parent forms, by applying transformation rules systematically.
Above and Beyond: Extended Learning (Section 3.E)
Learning Objectives for Above and Beyond – I can…
•  Simplify the difference quotient for a given function by substituting x+h, expanding, and fully simplifying the expression.
•  Interpret secant line limits graphically by connecting the behavior of the difference quotient as h approaches zero to the slope of the tangent line.
•  Evaluate secant line limits numerically by computing difference quotient values for smaller and smaller values of h and identifying the limiting value.
•  Draw tangent lines to curves at a given point and estimate instantaneous rate of change from the slope of the tangent line.

Module 04: Function Analysis and Domain/Range
Essential Standard
Students will identify domain and range from graphs, describe function behavior including increasing and decreasing intervals, intercepts, and extrema, classify functions as even or odd algebraically and graphically, state end behavior of polynomial and rational functions, and extend learning by evaluating limits as x approaches positive and negative infinity.
Section 4.A: Identify Domain and Range from Graphs
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Find the domain of a function from its graph by identifying all input values for which the function is defined, using interval notation.
•  Find the range of a function from its graph by identifying all output values the function produces, using interval notation.
•  Identify domain and range visually from a variety of graph types including continuous, discrete, and piecewise functions.
Section 4.B: Describe Functions — Increasing/Decreasing, Intercepts, Extrema
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Identify whether a function is increasing, decreasing, or constant on an interval and classify it as linear or nonlinear from its graph.
•  State the intervals on which a function is increasing or decreasing using interval notation, reasoning about the direction of the graph.
•  Identify x- and y-intercepts, local maxima, and local minima from a graph and distinguish between positive and negative intervals.
Section 4.C: Identify Even and Odd Functions
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Classify polynomial functions as even, odd, or neither by testing algebraic symmetry using f(−x) = f(x) or f(−x) = −f(x).
•  Identify even and odd functions from a graph by recognizing y-axis symmetry for even functions and origin symmetry for odd functions.
•  Use tables of values to determine whether a function is even, odd, or neither by checking for symmetry in the input-output pairs.
Section 4.D: State the End Behavior of a Polynomial or Rational Function
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Describe the end behavior of a function graphically using arrow notation, identifying what happens as x approaches positive and negative infinity.
•  Determine the end behavior of a polynomial or rational function algebraically from its leading term and degree.
•  Identify polynomial terminology including degree, leading coefficient, standard form, and classify polynomials by name and number of terms.
Above and Beyond: Extended Learning (Section 4.E)
Learning Objectives for Above and Beyond – I can…
•  Find limits as x approaches positive and negative infinity from a table of values by recognizing the value the function approaches.
•  Write limit notation for expressions as x approaches infinity, interpreting the behavior of functions from their algebraic form.
•  Write limits as x approaches infinity for logarithmic and exponential functions and interpret their long-run behavior.
•  Identify vertical and horizontal asymptotes as limits graphically, writing limit notation to describe behavior near asymptotes.

Module 05: Unit Circle and Trigonometric Functions
Essential Standard
Students will work with radians, degrees, and the unit circle including positive and negative angles, determine exact trigonometric values for special angles including all six functions, graph and write equations for transformations of sinusoidal functions, solve trigonometric equations on a specified interval, and extend learning through optimization problems involving area, perimeter, cost, and cubic situations.
Section 5.A: Work with Radians, Degrees, and the Unit Circle
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Convert between radians and degrees and rotate angles in both positive and negative directions on the coordinate plane.
•  Identify and place positive and negative angles in standard position, recognizing coterminal angles and direction of rotation.
•  Name an angle in standard position given a point on the unit circle, identifying the corresponding radian measure and quadrant.
Section 5.B: Determine the Sine, Cosine, and/or Tangent of Special Angles in the Unit Circle
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Determine exact values of sine and cosine for special angles in radians graphically using the unit circle.
•  Evaluate exact values of sine, cosine, and tangent for special angles in radians by applying unit circle structure and repeated reasoning.
•  Evaluate exact values of cosecant, secant, and cotangent for special angles in radians using reciprocal identities.
Section 5.C: Graph or Write the Equation for Transformations of a Sinusoidal Function
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Identify the period of a sinusoidal function from its graph by measuring the length of one complete cycle for sine and cosine.
•  Graph transformations of sinusoidal functions including vertical stretches and compressions, reflections, and vertical and horizontal shifts.
•  Write the equation of a sinusoidal function given its key features or graph, including amplitude, period, phase shift, and vertical shift.
Section 5.D: Solve Trigonometric Equations
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Solve basic trigonometric equations involving sine and cosine in radians on the interval [0, 2π) using unit circle reasoning.
•  Solve trigonometric equations that require factoring in a trinomial style by finding all solutions on [0, 2π).
•  Solve trigonometric equations involving secant and cosecant by converting to sine and cosine and solving on [0, 2π).
Above and Beyond: Extended Learning (Section 5.E)
Learning Objectives for Above and Beyond – I can…
•  Solve optimization problems involving area and perimeter by writing a function, finding its maximum or minimum, and interpreting the result in context.
•  Solve optimization problems involving cost by modeling the situation with a function and determining the input that minimizes or maximizes cost.
•  Solve optimization problems involving cubic situations by setting up the objective function, finding critical values, and justifying the optimal solution.

Module 08: Polynomial and Rational Expressions
Essential Standard
Students will factor polynomial expressions, perform operations with rational expressions, simplify complex fractions, and divide polynomials, connecting algebraic structure to procedural fluency while applying mathematical practices to model real-world situations and extend learning through limits.
Section 8.A: Factor Expressions
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Factor trinomial expressions using structural patterns including leading coefficient recognition (Level 1 and Level 2).
•  Factor expressions with a binomial greatest common factor by identifying and extracting common polynomial factors.
•  Look for and use the structure of polynomial expressions to choose and apply the most efficient factoring strategy.
Section 8.B: Complete Operations with Rational Expressions
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Simplify rational expressions by factoring numerators and denominators and canceling common factors, attending to restrictions on the variable.
•  Multiply and divide rational expressions using factoring and cancellation, reasoning abstractly about equivalent forms.
•  Add and subtract rational expressions by finding common denominators and combining numerators precisely.
Section 8.C: Simplify Complex Fractions
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Add and subtract fractions containing variables by identifying least common denominators and simplifying results.
•  Multiply and divide fractions with variables, including using complex fraction structure as a division strategy.

Section 8.D: Divide Polynomials
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Divide polynomials using long division, connecting the process to numerical long division and interpreting remainders.

Module 09: Rational Functions and Asymptotes
Essential Standard
Students will graph transformations of rational functions, identify horizontal, vertical, and slant asymptotes algebraically, analyze reciprocal functions and their key features, and solve polynomial equations — connecting algebraic structure to graphical behavior while applying mathematical practices to model real-world situations and extend learning through limits of rational functions.
Section 9.A: Graph Transformations of Rational Functions
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Graph basic rational functions by identifying key features including domain, range, and asymptotic behavior from the equation.
•  Graph rational functions by applying transformations and analyzing how parameters affect the shape and position of the graph.
•  Determine key features of a rational graph including intercepts, asymptotes, holes, and end behavior from its equation.
Section 9.B: Identify Horizontal, Vertical, and Slant Asymptotes
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Find vertical asymptotes algebraically by identifying values that make the denominator zero after factoring and canceling.
•  Find horizontal asymptotes algebraically by comparing the degrees of the numerator and denominator polynomials.
•  Find slant asymptotes using polynomial division and determine whether a rational function has a horizontal or slant asymptote.
Section 9.C: Graph Reciprocal Functions and Identify Their Asymptotes
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Find x-intercepts of rational functions algebraically by setting the numerator equal to zero and solving.
•  Find y-intercepts of rational functions algebraically by substituting x = 0 and simplifying.
•  Determine a complete set of features of a rational graph at Level 2, including intercepts, asymptotes, holes, and behavior near asymptotes.
Section 9.D: Solve and Graph Polynomial Equations
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Find all integer roots of a polynomial equation using the Integer Root Theorem and synthetic or long division to factor completely.
•  Find all roots of a polynomial equation including irrational roots by applying the Integer Root Theorem and the quadratic formula.
•  Write the equation of a polynomial function from its graph by identifying roots, multiplicities, and the leading coefficient.
Above and Beyond: Extended Learning (Section 9.E)
Learning Objectives for Above and Beyond – I can…
•  Find holes in rational functions algebraically by identifying factors that cancel from numerator and denominator.
•  Write limits from a graph of a rational function using correct limit notation, including one-sided and two-sided limits at holes.
•  Evaluate limits of rational functions using direct substitution when the function is defined at the point.
•  Evaluate limits of rational functions at holes by factoring, canceling, and substituting to find the limiting value.

Module 10: Exponential and Logarithmic Functions
Essential Standard
Students will rewrite expressions using properties of logarithms, graph transformations of exponential and logarithmic functions, solve exponential and logarithmic equations — including those with extraneous solutions — and extend learning by applying the Power Rule to find derivatives of polynomial functions.
Section 10.A: Rewrite Expressions Using the Properties of Logarithms
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Condense logarithmic expressions into a single logarithm by applying the product, quotient, and power properties.
•  Expand logarithmic expressions at Level 1 by applying the product, quotient, and power properties to rewrite a single logarithm.
•  Expand logarithmic expressions at Level 3, including complex expressions with roots and multiple factors, attending to precision.
Section 10.B: Graph Basic Transformations of the Family of Logarithmic Functions
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Identify and describe key features of exponential and logarithmic functions including domain, range, intercepts, and asymptotes.
•  Make a table of values, plot points, graph logarithmic functions at Level 1, and determine domain and range.
•  Graph exponential and logarithmic functions including transformations such as shifts, reflections, and stretches.
Section 10.C: Solve Equations with Exponents and Logarithms
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Solve exponential equations at Level 1 by rewriting both sides with a common base and applying properties of exponents.
•  Solve basic logarithmic equations by converting between logarithmic and exponential form.
•  Solve first-degree logarithmic equations by applying logarithm properties and isolating the variable.
Section 10.D: Solve Logarithmic Functions with Extraneous Solutions
Learning Objectives – I can…
•  Solve second-degree logarithmic equations at Level 1 by condensing, converting to exponential form, and solving the resulting quadratic.
•  Solve second-degree logarithmic equations at Level 2, including complex cases, and check all solutions for extraneous values.

Above and Beyond: Extended Learning (Section 10.E)
Learning Objectives for Above and Beyond – I can…
•  Find derivatives of polynomial functions by applying the Power Rule to each term and simplifying.

•  Apply the Power Rule at Level 3 to more complex polynomial and rational expressions, persevering through multi-step differentiation.
