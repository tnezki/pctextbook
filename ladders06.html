Module Outline 06: Polynomial Functions and Equations

Essential Standard
Students will understand polynomial functions and equations, including how to graph, factor, and solve them, and how to connect algebraic representations to graphical features while applying mathematical practices to model real-world situations and extend learning through series notation.

Section 6.A: Graph Quadratic Equations

Learning Objectives – I can...

  • Graph quadratic equations by identifying the vertex, axis of symmetry, and key points on parabolas.
  • Apply transformations to the parent function y = x² to sketch parabolas with vertical and horizontal shifts, reflections, and stretches.
  • Use technology strategically to verify my graphs and explore the relationships between parameters in different forms of quadratic equations.

Section 6.B: Sketch Graphs of Polynomial Functions in Factored Form

Learning Objectives – I can...

  • Sketch the graph of a polynomial function from its factored form, using roots to determine key features and behavior.
  • Create and interpret sign charts to analyze where polynomial functions are positive and negative, connecting structure to behavior.
  • Look for and use the structure of factored form to make predictions about the shape and key features of polynomial graphs.

Section 6.C: Connect Intercepts to Factors and Write Equations

Learning Objectives – I can...

  • Connect x and y intercepts on a graph to the factors and terms of a polynomial equation.
  • Write the equation of a polynomial function given its graph, reasoning abstractly about coefficients and factors.
  • Sketch polynomial graphs from sign charts and determine y-intercepts by attending to precision in algebraic computations.

Section 6.D: Determine Key Features of Polynomial Functions

Learning Objectives – I can...

  • Identify and interpret key features of polynomial functions, including degree, leading coefficient, x and y intercepts, and end behavior.
  • Determine whether polynomial functions exhibit even or odd degree behavior from their graphs and look for regularity in patterns.
  • Analyze the effects of degree, roots, and leading coefficient on the shape and position of polynomial graphs.

Above and Beyond: Extended Learning

Learning Objectives – I can...

  • Use sigma notation to express series and sums involving algebraic expressions, making sense of complex notation while persevering through multi-step problems.
  • Model real-world situations using polynomial functions, constructing viable arguments about appropriate models and justifying my reasoning with mathematical evidence.

LEARNING LADDER 06: POLYNOMIAL FUNCTIONS AND EQUATIONS

This learning ladder contains 7 rungs aligned with the 8 Math Practices. The bottom 5 rungs represent essential learning that average students should master. The top 2 rungs represent extended learning for advanced students.

Progress Tracking

Students self-assess on each rung using the following scale:

Not Yet: I need more instruction and practice
Getting There: I understand most of it but need more practice
Got It!: I can do this independently

Assessment Methods

Rung 1: Graphing activities with guided scaffolds and graphing technology
Rung 2: Transformation problems and function notation applications
Rung 3: Sketching from factored form and sign analysis
Rung 4: Writing equations from graphs and intercept identification
Rung 5: Feature identification worksheets and graphing analysis
Rung 6: Sigma notation application and series problem sets
Rung 7: Real-world modeling project and mathematical argumentation
