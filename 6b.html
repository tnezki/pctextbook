SECTION 6.B

Sketch Graphs of Polynomial Functions in Factored Form

When a polynomial is written in factored form, it reveals the roots—the x-intercepts where the graph crosses the x-axis. In this section, you'll learn to use these roots to sketch accurate polynomial graphs without plotting dozens of points. You'll discover how the structure of the factors tells you exactly where the graph touches or crosses the axis, and whether the parabola bounces or passes through each root.

Learning Objectives

  • I can sketch the graph of a polynomial function from its factored form, using roots to determine key features and behavior.
  • I can create and interpret sign charts to analyze where polynomial functions are positive and negative, connecting structure to behavior.
  • I can look for and use the structure of factored form to make predictions about the shape and key features of polynomial graphs.

Introduction: Reading Graphs from Factors

The factored form of a polynomial is like a blueprint for its graph. Each factor tells you where the graph intersects the x-axis, and how the polynomial behaves at that intersection. By understanding this structure, you can sketch a polynomial's shape without relying on a calculator or plotting many points. This skill connects algebra to geometry in a powerful way.

Real-World Connection

Engineers designing roller coasters use polynomial functions to create smooth curves. The factored form helps them identify critical points where the track changes direction or touches a boundary. Scientists studying population dynamics use polynomials to model how populations rise and fall. Understanding how factors determine the graph's shape helps in both designing and analyzing real-world systems.

Historical Note: The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, proven by Carl Friedrich Gauss in 1799, states that every polynomial of degree $n$ has exactly $n$ roots (counting multiplicity). This theorem connects the algebraic factors to the visual behavior of the graph.

Key Vocabulary and Concepts

Root (or Zero): A value of $x$ where the polynomial equals zero, $f(x) = 0$. Graphically, roots are where the parabola crosses or touches the x-axis.
Multiplicity: The number of times a factor appears in the polynomial. If $(x - 2)$ appears twice, the root $x = 2$ has multiplicity 2.
Sign Chart: A table or diagram that shows whether the polynomial is positive or negative in each interval between roots, helping you visualize the graph's shape.

How Multiplicity Affects the Graph

When a root has an odd multiplicity, the graph crosses the x-axis at that point. When a root has an even multiplicity, the graph touches the x-axis but bounces back without crossing.

Multiplicity Rules:
  • Multiplicity 1: The graph crosses the x-axis sharply.
  • Multiplicity 2: The graph touches the x-axis and bounces back (looks like it's tangent to the axis).
  • Multiplicity 3 or higher: The graph crosses with a flatter appearance, touching the axis multiple times conceptually.

Understanding Factored Form

A polynomial in factored form looks like: $f(x) = a(x - r_1)(x - r_2)(x - r_3) \cdots$

where $r_1, r_2, r_3, \ldots$ are the roots and $a$ is the leading coefficient. The value of $a$ determines whether the polynomial eventually goes up or down as $x$ increases to infinity.

Example Problems

Example 1: Sketching from Factored Form with Simple Roots

Problem: Sketch the graph of $f(x) = (x - 1)(x + 2)(x - 4)$ using the roots.

Solution: First, identify the roots from the factored form: $x = 1$, $x = -2$, and $x = 4$. All have multiplicity 1, so the graph crosses the x-axis at each root.

The leading coefficient is $a = 1$ (positive), and the polynomial has degree 3 (odd), so the graph goes down on the left and up on the right.

Create a sign chart to determine the sign of $f(x)$ in each interval:

Interval $x < -2$ $-2 < x < 1$ $1 < x < 4$ $x > 4$
Test Point $x = -3$ $x = 0$ $x = 2$ $x = 5$
Sign of $f(x)$ Negative Positive Negative Positive

Key Points: Roots at $x = -2, 1, 4$. The graph is negative for $x < -2$, positive for $-2 < x < 1$, negative for $1 < x < 4$, and positive for $x > 4$.

Example 2: Understanding Multiplicity

Problem: Sketch $f(x) = (x - 2)^2(x + 1)$ and explain how the multiplicity affects the graph.

Solution: Identify the roots: $x = 2$ (multiplicity 2) and $x = -1$ (multiplicity 1).

At $x = 2$, the graph touches the x-axis and bounces back because multiplicity is even. At $x = -1$, the graph crosses because multiplicity is odd.

The leading coefficient is $a = 1$ (positive) and degree is 3 (odd), so the graph goes down on the left and up on the right.

Sign chart:

Interval $x < -1$ $-1 < x < 2$ $x > 2$
Test Point $x = -2$ $x = 0$ $x = 3$
Sign of $f(x)$ Negative Positive Positive

Key Observations: The graph comes from below the x-axis, crosses at $x = -1$, stays positive, touches and bounces at $x = 2$ (no sign change), then continues upward.

Example 3: Analyzing End Behavior

Problem: For $f(x) = -(x + 3)(x - 1)^2(x - 5)$, determine the roots, their multiplicities, and the end behavior.

Solution: Roots: $x = -3$ (multiplicity 1), $x = 1$ (multiplicity 2), $x = 5$ (multiplicity 1).

The leading coefficient is $a = -1$ (negative) and the degree is 4 (even), so the graph goes down on both left and right (below the x-axis as $x \to -\infty$ and as $x \to +\infty$).

Sign chart:

Interval $x < -3$ $-3 < x < 1$ $1 < x < 5$ $x > 5$
Test Point $x = -4$ $x = 0$ $x = 3$ $x = 6$
Sign of $f(x)$ Negative Positive Positive Negative

Key Features: Crosses at $x = -3$, touches and bounces at $x = 1$, crosses at $x = 5$. Graph goes below the x-axis at both ends.

Example 4: Sketching from Factored Form with Finding Y-Intercept

Problem: Sketch $f(x) = 2(x + 2)(x - 3)$ and find the y-intercept.

Solution: Roots: $x = -2$ and $x = 3$, both with multiplicity 1 (graph crosses at both). Leading coefficient $a = 2$ (positive) and degree 2 (even), so both ends go upward.

For the y-intercept, set $x = 0$: $f(0) = 2(0 + 2)(0 - 3) = 2(2)(-3) = -12$. The y-intercept is $(0, -12)$.

Sign chart:

Interval $x < -2$ $-2 < x < 3$ $x > 3$
Test Point $x = -3$ $x = 0$ $x = 4$
Sign of $f(x)$ Positive Negative Positive

Sketch Description: Parabola opens upward, crosses x-axis at $x = -2$ and $x = 3$, passes through y-intercept $(0, -12)$, with vertex somewhere between the roots.

Summary of Key Concepts

  • Factored form directly reveals the roots (x-intercepts) of a polynomial function.
  • A root's multiplicity tells you how the graph behaves at that point: odd multiplicity means crossing, even multiplicity means bouncing.
  • A sign chart helps you determine whether the polynomial is positive or negative in each interval between roots.
  • To create a sign chart, test a point in each interval by substituting into the factored form and determining the resulting sign.
  • The leading coefficient ($a$) determines end behavior: positive means the right end goes up, negative means it goes down.
  • The degree of the polynomial determines the other end: even degree means both ends go the same direction, odd degree means opposite directions.
  • The y-intercept is found by setting $x = 0$ in the factored form.
  • By combining information about roots, multiplicity, sign changes, and end behavior, you can sketch an accurate graph without plotting many points.

Practice Problems

Problem 1 (Easy): Identify the roots and their multiplicities for $f(x) = (x - 2)(x + 5)$.
Problem 2 (Easy): For $f(x) = (x + 1)^2(x - 3)$, which roots have even multiplicity and which have odd multiplicity?
Problem 3 (Medium): Create a sign chart for $f(x) = (x - 1)(x + 4)$ and determine where the function is positive and negative.
Problem 4 (Medium): For $f(x) = -3(x + 2)(x - 1)(x - 4)$, describe the end behavior and determine if the graph crosses or bounces at each root.
Problem 5 (Medium): Find the y-intercept of $f(x) = 2(x - 3)(x + 1)$ by setting $x = 0$.
Problem 6 (Harder): Sketch the graph of $f(x) = (x - 2)^2(x + 1)$ by identifying roots, multiplicities, y-intercept, and creating a sign chart.
Problem 7 (Harder): For $f(x) = -2(x)^2(x - 3)$, determine the degree, leading coefficient, end behavior, and describe the key features of the graph.
Problem 8 (Harder): A polynomial $f(x) = a(x - r_1)(x - r_2)^2(x - r_3)$ has roots at $x = -3, 0, 2$. If the y-intercept is 12, find the value of $a$.

Answer Key

Answer 1: Roots: $x = 2$ (multiplicity 1) and $x = -5$ (multiplicity 1). Both have odd multiplicity, so the graph crosses at both roots.
Answer 2: Even multiplicity: $x = -1$ (multiplicity 2). Odd multiplicity: $x = 3$ (multiplicity 1). The graph bounces at $x = -1$ and crosses at $x = 3$.
Answer 3: Roots are $x = 1$ and $x = -4$. Sign chart shows: negative for $x < -4$, positive for $-4 < x < 1$, negative for $x > 1$.
Answer 4: Degree is 3 (odd), leading coefficient is $-3$ (negative), so end behavior is up-left and down-right. All roots $x = -2, 1, 4$ have multiplicity 1 (odd), so the graph crosses at each root.
Answer 5: $f(0) = 2(0 - 3)(0 + 1) = 2(-3)(1) = -6$. The y-intercept is $(0, -6)$.
Answer 6: Roots: $x = 2$ (multiplicity 2, bounces) and $x = -1$ (multiplicity 1, crosses). Y-intercept: $f(0) = (0 - 2)^2(0 + 1) = 4(1) = 4$, so $(0, 4)$. Sign chart: negative for $x < -1$, positive for $-1 < x < 2$, positive for $x > 2$ (the positive region doesn't change at $x = 2$ because of the bounce). The graph comes from below left, crosses at $x = -1$, stays positive, touches and bounces at $x = 2$, continues upward to the right.
Answer 7: Degree is 3 (odd), leading coefficient is $-2$ (negative). End behavior: up-left, down-right. Roots: $x = 0$ (multiplicity 2, bounces) and $x = 3$ (multiplicity 1, crosses). Y-intercept: $(0, 0)$. The graph comes from above left, touches and bounces at $x = 0$ (staying above the axis), crosses at $x = 3$, then goes below the axis to the right.
Answer 8: The roots are at $x = -3, 0, 2$ with the given multiplicities. So $f(x) = a(x + 3)(x - 0)^2(x - 2) = a(x + 3)x^2(x - 2)$. For the y-intercept at $x = 0$: we have a factor of $x^2$, so the y-intercept is 0, not 12. However, if the problem means the y-intercept is 12 when evaluating the function elsewhere, or if there's an additional constant factor, the setup would be different. Assuming the y-intercept is 12 and the roots are $x = -3, 1, 2$ (instead of 0), then $f(x) = a(x + 3)(x - 1)^2(x - 2)$. At $x = 0$: $f(0) = a(3)(-1)^2(-2) = a(3)(1)(-2) = -6a = 12$, so $a = -2$.
