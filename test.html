9.A Graph Transformations of Rational Functions

Rational functions exhibit predictable structural behavior governed by algebraic transformations. Rather than relying on point-by-point plotting, effective analysis depends on recognizing how transformations alter the fundamental structure of a parent function.
By interpreting parameters within the function, we can determine asymptotes, orientation, and relative position with precision. This allows for efficient graphing and deeper conceptual understanding.
“Mathematics reveals structure — it does not create it.”

Learning Targets

I can identify horizontal and vertical transformations of rational functions.

I can determine the effects of reflections and stretches/compressions.

I can sketch transformed rational functions using structural reasoning.

Introduction

The parent rational function is:

$$ f(x)=\frac{1}{x} $$

This function is defined for all real values of $x$ except $x=0$. At this value, the function is undefined, creating a vertical asymptote. The graph also approaches, but never touches, the horizontal asymptote at $y=0$.

Unlike polynomial functions, rational functions do not form continuous curves. Instead, they consist of separate branches that approach asymptotes. These asymptotes define the structure of the graph and guide how it behaves.

Key Idea: Transformations do not change the fundamental structure of a rational function. They shift and reshape the graph while preserving its asymptotic behavior.

Key Concepts

General Form of a Transformed Rational Function

$$ f(x)=\frac{a}{x-h}+k $$
Parameters and Their Effects:
  • a → controls vertical stretch/compression and reflection
  • h → shifts the graph left or right
  • k → shifts the graph up or down

Asymptotes

  • Vertical Asymptote: $x = h$
  • Horizontal Asymptote: $y = k$

Transformation Summary

  • $x - h$ → shift right $h$ units
  • $x + h$ → shift left $h$ units
  • $+k$ → shift up $k$ units
  • $-k$ → shift down $k$ units
  • $a > 1$ → vertical stretch
  • $0 < a < 1$ → vertical compression
  • $a < 0$ → reflection across the x-axis

Structural Behavior

Rational functions based on $\frac{1}{x}$ always have two branches. These branches are separated by the vertical asymptote and approach both asymptotes without ever touching them.

Strategies

Graphing Strategy for Rational Functions:

  1. Identify asymptotes first.
    Determine the vertical asymptote from the denominator and the horizontal asymptote from the constant shift.
  2. Analyze transformations.
    Identify shifts ($h$, $k$), reflections, and stretches using the parameters in the function.
  3. Determine branch orientation.
    Use the sign of $a$ to determine which quadrants the graph will occupy relative to the asymptotes.
  4. Sketch relative to asymptotes.
    Draw branches approaching asymptotes without touching them. Focus on structure rather than plotting many points.
  5. Verify with key points (if needed).
    Use one or two points to confirm accuracy, not to build the entire graph.

Common Mistakes

  • Ignoring asymptotes:
    Students often try to graph the function without first identifying vertical and horizontal asymptotes, which leads to incorrect structure.
  • Reversing horizontal shifts:
    Remember that $x - h$ shifts right and $x + h$ shifts left. This is opposite of what many expect.
  • Forgetting reflections:
    A negative value of $a$ reflects the graph across the x-axis, changing the orientation of the branches.
  • Over-relying on plotting points:
    Rational functions should be sketched using asymptotes and structure, not built point-by-point.
  • Drawing branches that cross asymptotes:
    Graphs should approach asymptotes but never touch or cross them.

Summary

  • Rational functions are transformations of the parent function $\\frac{1}{x}$.
  • Asymptotes define the structure of the graph.
  • The parameters $a$, $h$, and $k$ control reflection, stretch, and translation.
  • Graphing should begin with identifying asymptotes before sketching.
  • Understanding structure allows you to graph efficiently without relying on tables of values.

Detailed Example Solutions

Example 1 — Horizontal Shift

Given:

$$ f(x)=\frac{1}{x-2} $$

Step 1: Identify asymptotes

Vertical asymptote occurs when the denominator is zero:

$$ x-2=0 \Rightarrow x=2 $$

Horizontal asymptote:

$$ y=0 $$

Step 2: Describe transformation

The graph is shifted right 2 units from $\\frac{1}{x}$.

Final Answer:

VA: $x=2$, HA: $y=0$

Example 2 — Vertical Translation

Given:

$$ f(x)=\frac{1}{x}+3 $$

Step 1: Identify asymptotes

Vertical asymptote:

$$ x=0 $$

Horizontal asymptote:

$$ y=3 $$

Step 2: Describe transformation

The graph is shifted up 3 units.

Final Answer:

VA: $x=0$, HA: $y=3$

Example 3 — Reflection Across the x-axis

Given:

$$ f(x)=-\frac{1}{x} $$

Step 1: Identify asymptotes

Vertical asymptote:

$$ x=0 $$

Horizontal asymptote:

$$ y=0 $$

Step 2: Describe transformation

The negative sign reflects the graph across the x-axis.

Final Answer:

VA: $x=0$, HA: $y=0$, reflection across x-axis

Example 4 — Vertical Stretch

Given:

$$ f(x)=\frac{2}{x} $$

Step 1: Identify asymptotes

Vertical asymptote:

$$ x=0 $$

Horizontal asymptote:

$$ y=0 $$

Step 2: Describe transformation

The factor of 2 creates a vertical stretch.

Final Answer:

VA: $x=0$, HA: $y=0$, stretch factor 2

Example 5 — Combined Transformations

Given:

$$ f(x)=\frac{1}{x+4}-1 $$

Step 1: Identify asymptotes

Vertical asymptote:

$$ x+4=0 \Rightarrow x=-4 $$

Horizontal asymptote:

$$ y=-1 $$

Step 2: Describe transformation

The graph shifts left 4 units and down 1 unit.

Final Answer:

VA: $x=-4$, HA: $y=-1$

Example 6 — Reflection and Translation

Given:

$$ f(x)=-\frac{3}{x-1}+2 $$

Step 1: Identify asymptotes

Vertical asymptote:

$$ x-1=0 \Rightarrow x=1 $$

Horizontal asymptote:

$$ y=2 $$

Step 2: Describe transformation

The graph is shifted right 1 unit and up 2 units.

The negative sign reflects the graph across the x-axis.

The factor of 3 creates a vertical stretch.

Final Answer:

VA: $x=1$, HA: $y=2$, reflection, stretch factor 3
