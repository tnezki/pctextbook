10.C Solving Exponential and Logarithmic Equations

Exponential and logarithmic equations allow us to solve for unknown exponents and understand relationships involving growth, decay, and scaling.
Solving these equations requires recognizing structure, rewriting expressions, and applying inverse relationships between exponential and logarithmic forms.
“To solve exponential equations, reveal the structure. To solve logarithmic equations, reverse it.”

Learning Targets

I can convert between exponential and logarithmic forms.

I can solve logarithmic equations using properties of logs.

Introduction

Solving exponential and logarithmic equations involves reversing operations. Exponential equations require isolating exponents, while logarithmic equations require rewriting expressions and applying properties.

Some equations can be solved by rewriting both sides with a common base, while others require converting between logarithmic and exponential forms.

Key Idea: Solving equations depends on recognizing structure and using inverse relationships.

History

Exponential equations have been studied since early algebra, particularly in relation to growth and compounding. Logarithms were later introduced as a way to simplify these calculations.

Mathematicians realized that solving exponential equations could be simplified by using logarithms, leading to a deeper understanding of inverse relationships.

Today, these techniques are fundamental in algebra and are widely used in science, engineering, and economics.

Key Concepts

Solving Exponential Equations

Exponential equations can be solved by rewriting both sides with a common base.

$$ a^x = a^n \Rightarrow x = n $$

Example:

$$ 2^x = 8 \Rightarrow 2^x = 2^3 \Rightarrow x=3 $$

Using a Common Base

Rewrite expressions so both sides have the same base before solving.

$$ 9^x = 3^{2x} \Rightarrow (3^2)^x = 3^{2x} $$

Simplify and compare exponents.

Converting Logarithmic Form

Logarithmic equations can be rewritten in exponential form.

$$ \log_b(x) = y \Rightarrow b^y = x $$

Example:

$$ \log_2 x = 3 \Rightarrow 2^3 = x \Rightarrow x=8 $$

Solving Logarithmic Equations

Logarithmic equations can be solved by rewriting or applying properties.

$$ \log x = 2 \Rightarrow x = 100 $$

Convert to exponential form and solve.

Applying Logarithm Properties

Use properties to combine or simplify expressions before solving.

$$ \log x + \log(x-1) = \log 20 $$

Combine:

$$ \log[x(x-1)] = \log 20 $$ $$ x(x-1)=20 $$

Isolating the Variable

After rewriting, solve using algebraic techniques.

$$ x^2 - x - 20 = 0 $$

Factor and solve for \(x\).

Multi-Step Exponential Equations

Some equations require rewriting and simplifying before solving.

$$ 2^{x+1} = 16 $$

Rewrite:

$$ 2^{x+1} = 2^4 $$ $$ x+1=4 \Rightarrow x=3 $$

Multi-Step Logarithmic Equations

Use properties and algebra together to solve.

$$ \log(x+3) + \log(x-1) = \log 24 $$

Combine and solve:

$$ (x+3)(x-1)=24 $$

Mixed Equations

When no common base exists, use logarithms to solve.

$$ 2^x = 7 $$

Take logarithm:

$$ x = \log_2 7 $$

Using Natural Logarithms

Natural logarithms are used when the base is \(e\).

$$ e^x = 5 \Rightarrow x = \ln 5 $$

Checking Solutions

All solutions must be checked to ensure they satisfy the original equation.

$$ \log(x-1) \Rightarrow x>1 $$
Important: Logarithmic equations require checking domain restrictions.

Vocabulary

Exponential Equation:

An equation where the variable appears in the exponent.

Logarithmic Equation:

An equation involving logarithmic expressions.

Common Base:

Rewriting expressions so both sides have the same base.

Equivalent Forms:

Different representations of the same relationship, such as logarithmic and exponential forms.

Natural Logarithm:

A logarithm with base \(e\), written as \(\ln x\).

Domain Restriction:

A condition that limits valid solutions, especially in logarithmic expressions.

Strategies

Solving Exponential Equations:

  1. Rewrite both sides using a common base if possible
  2. Set exponents equal
  3. Solve algebraically

Solving Logarithmic Equations:

  1. Combine logs using properties if needed
  2. Rewrite in exponential form
  3. Solve the resulting equation

When No Common Base Exists:

  1. Apply logarithms to both sides
  2. Solve for the variable
Key Idea: Always simplify structure before solving.

Common Mistakes

  • Not rewriting with a common base:
    Missing this step makes exponential equations harder than they need to be.
  • Incorrect conversion between forms:
    Confusing logarithmic and exponential relationships leads to errors.
  • Not combining logarithms first:
    Equations must often be simplified before solving.
  • Algebra mistakes after rewriting:
    Factoring and solving errors occur after structure is revealed.
  • Ignoring domain restrictions:
    Logarithmic expressions require positive arguments.
  • Stopping too early:
    Students may rewrite but not fully solve.

Summary

  • Exponential equations can be solved by rewriting with a common base.
  • Logarithmic equations can be converted to exponential form.
  • Logarithm properties help simplify expressions before solving.
  • Some equations require multiple steps and algebraic manipulation.
  • When no common base exists, logarithms can be used to solve.
  • Solutions must be checked for validity.

Detailed Example Solutions

Example 1 — Common Base

$$ 2^x = 32 $$

Rewrite:

$$ 32 = 2^5 $$ $$ 2^x = 2^5 $$

Set exponents equal:

$$ x=5 $$

Example 2 — Rewrite and Solve

$$ 9^x = 3^4 $$

Rewrite:

$$ 9 = 3^2 $$ $$ (3^2)^x = 3^4 $$ $$ 3^{2x} = 3^4 $$

Solve:

$$ 2x=4 \Rightarrow x=2 $$

Example 3 — Convert to Exponential Form

$$ \log_2 x = 5 $$

Rewrite:

$$ 2^5 = x $$ $$ x=32 $$

Example 4 — Natural Logarithm

$$ \ln x = 2 $$

Rewrite:

$$ e^2 = x $$

Example 5 — Log Properties

$$ \log x + \log(x-1) = \log 20 $$

Combine:

$$ \log[x(x-1)] = \log 20 $$

Rewrite:

$$ x(x-1)=20 $$ $$ x^2 - x - 20 = 0 $$ $$ (x-5)(x+4)=0 $$

Solutions:

$$ x=5, -4 $$

Example 6 — Multi-Step Equation

$$ 2^{x+1} = 16 $$

Rewrite:

$$ 16 = 2^4 $$ $$ 2^{x+1} = 2^4 $$

Solve:

$$ x+1=4 \Rightarrow x=3 $$

Example 7 — No Common Base

$$ 2^x = 7 $$

Take logarithm:

$$ x = \log_2 7 $$
