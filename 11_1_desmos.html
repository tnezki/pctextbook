11.1 Power Rule

Investigation

Graph unusual power-rule functions, estimate tangent lines at two input values, and test your line equations by graphing them.
How This Investigation Works

For each function:

  • Graph the function in Desmos.
  • Find the point on the graph at each given input value.
  • Estimate the slope of the tangent line by looking carefully at the graph.
  • Write an equation for your estimated tangent line. Name them \(L_1(x)\) and \(L_2(x)\).
  • Enter your estimated lines into Desmos to test how closely they match the curve near the point of tangency.
  • Revise your estimate if needed.

Suggested Desmos setup:

f(x)= ...
L_1(x)= ...
L_2(x)= ...

Do not use derivative commands. The goal is to estimate the slope visually, use the point on the graph, and practice writing the line yourself.

These functions are intentionally more unusual than the ones from class notes. Pay attention to where the graph is steep, flat, increasing, decreasing, or changing shape.

Functions 1 and 2

Function 1

\(f(x)=x^{5/3}-2x^{1/3}\)

Estimate the tangent lines at \(x=-1\) and \(x=8\).

f(x)=x^(5/3)-2x^(1/3)

Write your estimated equations for \(L_1(x)\) and \(L_2(x)\), then graph them.

Function 2

\(g(x)=3x^{1/2}-x^2\)

Estimate the tangent lines at \(x=1\) and \(x=4\).

g(x)=3x^(1/2)-x^2

After graphing, decide whether your line looks too steep, too flat, or close to correct.

Functions 3 and 4

Function 3

\(h(x)=x^{-2}+2x\)

Estimate the tangent lines at \(x=1\) and \(x=2\).

h(x)=x^(-2)+2x

Be careful near values where the graph changes steepness quickly.

Function 4

\(p(x)=2x^{3/2}-5x^{1/2}\)

Estimate the tangent lines at \(x=1\) and \(x=9\).

p(x)=2x^(3/2)-5x^(1/2)

Use the graph to judge whether the slope should be positive, negative, or near zero.

Functions 5 and 6

Function 5

\(q(x)=x^{4/3}-4x^{1/3}\)

Estimate the tangent lines at \(x=1\) and \(x=8\).

q(x)=x^(4/3)-4x^(1/3)

Write the equations in line form, not just point-slope form.

Function 6

\(r(x)=x^{-1/2}+x^{3/2}\)

Estimate the tangent lines at \(x=1\) and \(x=4\).

r(x)=x^(-1/2)+x^(3/2)

Notice how the negative exponent affects the graph near smaller positive \(x\)-values.

Functions 7 and 8

Function 7

\(s(x)=5x^{2/3}-x^{5/3}\)

Estimate the tangent lines at \(x=1\) and \(x=8\).

s(x)=5x^(2/3)-x^(5/3)

Compare the local shape of the graph at the two points before writing your lines.

Function 8

\(t(x)=2x^{-3}+x^2\)

Estimate the tangent lines at \(x=-1\) and \(x=1\).

t(x)=2x^(-3)+x^2

The graph behaves very differently on the two sides. Let that guide your estimate.

Functions 9 and 10

Function 9

\(u(x)=x^{7/3}-3x^{4/3}\)

Estimate the tangent lines at \(x=1\) and \(x=8\).

u(x)=x^(7/3)-3x^(4/3)

After graphing your lines, decide where your estimate was strongest and where it needed adjustment.

Function 10

\(v(x)=4x^{-1}+x^3\)

Estimate the tangent lines at \(x=1\) and \(x=2\).

v(x)=4x^(-1)+x^3

Think carefully about how the graph bends as the cubic term begins to dominate.

Discussion and Reflection
  1. Which functions were easiest to estimate tangent lines for, and what made them easier to read from the graph?
  2. Which functions were hardest to estimate, and why?
  3. What graph features made it easier to tell whether a tangent line should have a positive slope, a negative slope, or a slope near zero?
  4. How did negative exponents affect the shape of the graph and your ability to estimate the slope?
  5. How did fractional exponents affect the shape of the graph and your ability to estimate the slope?
  6. When your estimated line and the graph did not match well, what usually needed to change more: the point, the slope, or the equation form?
  7. How did you use a point on the graph together with an estimated slope to write the tangent line equation?
  8. What clues helped you decide whether a line looked tangent to the curve rather than just crossing the graph nearby?
