SECTION 8.D

Dividing Polynomials

Polynomial division extends the structure of numerical long division into algebra. The same logical steps apply: divide, multiply, subtract, and repeat.
“Division reveals structure hidden inside higher-degree polynomials.”

Learning Objectives

  • I can divide polynomials using long division.
  • I can apply synthetic division when dividing by linear binomials.
  • I can use the box method to organize polynomial division.
  • I can interpret remainders using the Division Algorithm.
  • I can connect polynomial division to rational expressions.

Introduction: Extending Division to Algebra

Polynomial division follows the same structure as numerical division. You determine how many times the divisor fits into the leading term of the dividend, multiply, subtract, and repeat the process.

Unlike factoring, division does not break an expression apart — it reorganizes it.

When division is complete, the result may include:

  • An exact quotient (no remainder), or
  • A remainder polynomial.

When a remainder exists, it is written as a fraction over the original divisor.

Division Algorithm: \[ P(x) = D(x)Q(x) + R(x) \]

Here:

  • $P(x)$ is the dividend
  • $D(x)$ is the divisor
  • $Q(x)$ is the quotient
  • $R(x)$ is the remainder

Key Vocabulary

Dividend: The polynomial being divided.
Divisor: The polynomial you divide by.
Quotient: The result of division.
Remainder: The polynomial left after subtraction is complete.
Synthetic Division: A shortcut algorithm used when dividing by a linear binomial of the form $x - c$.

Core Methods

There are three primary methods for dividing polynomials:

  1. Long Division — mirrors numerical long division step by step.
  2. Box Method — organizes multiplication and subtraction visually.
  3. Synthetic Division — efficient for divisors of the form $x - c$.
Each method produces the same result. Choice of method depends on divisor structure and clarity.

Structural Preparation

Before dividing:

  • Rewrite the dividend in descending powers.
  • Insert zero placeholders for missing terms.
  • Ensure the divisor is written in standard form.

These steps prevent alignment errors and incorrect subtraction.

Worked Examples

Example 1 — Long Division

Divide:

$$ \frac{x^3 - 2x^2 + 4x - 8}{x - 2} $$

Step 1: Divide leading terms.

$$ \frac{x^3}{x} = x^2 $$

Step 2: Multiply and subtract.

Multiply: $$ x^2(x - 2) = x^3 - 2x^2 $$ Subtract from dividend: $$ (x^3 - 2x^2 + 4x - 8) - (x^3 - 2x^2) = 4x - 8 $$

Step 3: Repeat.

$$ \frac{4x}{x} = 4 $$ Multiply: $$ 4(x - 2) = 4x - 8 $$ Subtract: $$ (4x - 8) - (4x - 8) = 0 $$

Final Answer:

$$ x^2 + 4 $$

Example 2 — Box Method

Divide:

$$ \frac{2x^3 + 3x^2 - 5x + 6}{x + 2} $$

Step 1: Divide leading terms.

$$ \frac{2x^3}{x} = 2x^2 $$ Multiply: $$ 2x^2(x + 2) = 2x^3 + 4x^2 $$ Subtract: $$ (2x^3 + 3x^2) - (2x^3 + 4x^2) = -x^2 $$ Bring down remaining terms: $$ -x^2 - 5x $$

Step 2: Repeat.

$$ \frac{-x^2}{x} = -x $$ Multiply: $$ -x(x + 2) = -x^2 - 2x $$ Subtract: $$ (-x^2 - 5x) - (-x^2 - 2x) = -3x $$ Bring down +6: $$ -3x + 6 $$

Step 3: Final term.

$$ \frac{-3x}{x} = -3 $$ Multiply: $$ -3(x + 2) = -3x - 6 $$ Subtract: $$ (-3x + 6) - (-3x - 6) = 12 $$

Final Answer:

$$ 2x^2 - x - 3 + \frac{12}{x + 2} $$

Example 3 — Synthetic Division

Divide:

$$ \frac{x^4 - 3x^3 + 2x^2 - x + 5}{x - 1} $$

Step 1: Set up coefficients.

Coefficients: $$ 1 \quad -3 \quad 2 \quad -1 \quad 5 $$ Use $c = 1$.

Step 2: Perform synthetic division.

Bring down 1. Multiply and add across: Resulting coefficients: $$ 1 \quad -2 \quad 0 \quad -1 $$ Remainder: $$ 4 $$

Final Answer:

$$ x^3 - 2x^2 - 1 + \frac{4}{x - 1} $$

Example 4 — Missing Term Placeholder

Divide:

$$ \frac{x^3 + 4x - 5}{x - 2} $$
Rewrite with placeholder: $$ x^3 + 0x^2 + 4x - 5 $$ Using synthetic division with $c = 2$: Coefficients: $$ 1 \quad 0 \quad 4 \quad -5 $$ Result: $$ 1 \quad 2 \quad 8 $$ Remainder: $$ 11 $$

Final Answer:

$$ x^2 + 2x + 8 + \frac{11}{x - 2} $$

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting Missing Terms:
    If a polynomial skips a power (for example, no $x^2$ term), a zero placeholder must be inserted before dividing. Without placeholders, terms will not align correctly.
  • Sign Errors During Subtraction:
    When subtracting the product of the divisor, every term must change sign. Many errors occur from incomplete distribution of the negative sign.
  • Stopping Too Early:
    Division continues until the degree of the remainder is less than the degree of the divisor.
  • Using Synthetic Division Incorrectly:
    Synthetic division applies only when dividing by a linear binomial of the form $x - c$. It does not apply to quadratic or higher-degree divisors.
  • Dropping the Remainder:
    If the division is not exact, the remainder must be written as a fraction over the original divisor.
  • Failing to Write the Final Answer Properly:
    The final result must follow the Division Algorithm format: \[ Q(x) + \frac{R(x)}{D(x)} \]

Summary of Polynomial Division

Structural Situation Best Method Key Reminder
Any polynomial divisor Long Division Divide, multiply, subtract, repeat.
Visual organization preferred Box Method Use structured grid alignment.
Divisor of the form $x - c$ Synthetic Division Use coefficient-only shortcut.
Missing powers Insert zero placeholders Maintain correct term alignment.
Nonzero remainder Write fractional remainder Use Division Algorithm form.

Core Structural Principles:
  • Polynomial division mirrors numerical long division.
  • Every subtraction step must be exact and complete.
  • Synthetic division is an algorithmic shortcut, not a different result.
  • The Division Algorithm guarantees: \[ P(x) = D(x)Q(x) + R(x) \]
  • If $R(x)=0$, the divisor is a factor.

Solutions

Level 1 — Basic Division

1. $$ \frac{x^2 + 7x + 10}{x + 5} = \frac{(x+5)(x+2)}{x+5} = x + 2 \quad (x \ne -5) $$

2. $$ \frac{x^3 - 9x}{x} = \frac{x(x^2 - 9)}{x} = x^2 - 9 \quad (x \ne 0) $$

3. Synthetic division with $c=3$ gives: $$ x^2 + x - 2 $$

4. Include placeholder: $$ x^3 + 0x^2 + 2x - 4 $$ Long division yields: $$ x^2 + x + 3 - \frac{1}{x - 1} $$

Level 2 — Include Placeholders

5. Rewrite: $$ x^4 + 0x^3 + 3x^2 + 0x - 2 $$ Synthetic division with $c=2$ yields: $$ x^3 + 2x^2 + 7x + 14 + \frac{26}{x - 2} $$

6. Synthetic division with $c=2$ yields: $$ x^3 - 3x^2 - 6x - 8 + \frac{-24}{x - 2} $$

7. Long division yields: $$ 2x^2 - x - 6 + \frac{9}{x + 1} $$

8. Synthetic division with $c=1$ yields: $$ 3x^3 + 3x^2 + x + 6 - \frac{0}{x - 1} $$

Level 3 — Higher Degree and Remainders

9. $$ \frac{x^4 - 1}{x - 1} = x^3 + x^2 + x + 1 $$

10. Long division yields: $$ 2x^3 - 5x^2 + 10x - 17 + \frac{29}{x + 2} $$

11. Synthetic division with $c=1$ yields: $$ x^4 - x^3 - x^2 + 0x - 3 + \frac{1}{x - 1} $$

12. $$ \frac{x^4 - 6x^2 + 8}{x^2 - 2} = x^2 - 4 + \frac{0}{x^2 - 2} $$

Level 4 — Structural Interpretation

13. Long division yields: $$ x^2 + 2x + 0 + \frac{1}{x - 2} $$

14. Use synthetic division with $c=1$: Remainder is: $$ -5 $$ Therefore, $x - 1$ is not a factor.

15. By the Remainder Theorem: $$ P(3) = 5 $$

Level 5 — Justify

16. If the remainder had equal or higher degree than the divisor, further division would still be possible. Division stops only when the remainder's degree is smaller.

17. Synthetic division is a condensed form of long division where multiplication and subtraction steps are encoded into coefficient operations. Both follow the Division Algorithm.

18. Polynomial division allows a rational function to be rewritten as: $$ Q(x) + \frac{R(x)}{D(x)} $$ which separates the function into a polynomial plus a proper rational remainder.
